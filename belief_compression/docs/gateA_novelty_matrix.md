# Gate A — Prior-Art / Novelty Audit

**Method under audit:** "Decision-Equivalent Belief Compression for Scalable Active World-Model Planning"
**Date:** 2026-07-22
**Scope:** three claimed pillars — (1) regret-preserving compression bound for clustering K belief-hypotheses into M decision modes by decision-signature (goal-conditioned action-ranking) agreement; (2) amortized decision-regret VOI that predicts whether a probing observation would flip the preferred action, without nested inner planning; (3) goal-generalization of the compression across a family of goals, not one fixed reward.

Method restated in one line: cluster particles not by physical-state similarity but by whether they *induce the same action preference for every goal in a goal family*, prove this preserves near-optimal planning value, and use the compressed representation to amortize probing-action VOI.

## Pillar-by-pillar honest verdicts

**Pillar 1 — regret-preserving compression bound (cluster by decision-signature agreement, bound regret by ε and horizon).**
Largely **not novel as a bound**, though the specific *criterion* (goal-conditioned action-ranking-vector agreement across a family of goals) is not exactly what any single paper proves. Two independent, mature bodies of work already deliver value/regret bounds for belief-representation compression: (a) bisimulation metrics for continuous (PO)MDPs (Ferns et al., Castro et al., DeepMDP), which bound |V\*(s)−V\*(s′)| by a Wasserstein-style state metric, and (b) — the far bigger threat — the Technion ANPL "Simplified POMDP" program (Barenboim, Lev-Yehudi, Indelman), which explicitly frames *any* POMDP-component replacement (belief/particle aggregation, observation-model simplification, reward simplification) as a "simplification" and proves formal, planner-realized (not just abstract V\*) performance/regret bounds, up to literal "same action selected" guarantees, including for continuous/camera-image observation models. **Strongest threat to pillar 1: "Simplifying Complex Observation Models in Continuous POMDP Planning with Probabilistic Guarantees and Practice" (Lev-Yehudi, Barenboim & Indelman, AAAI 2024, arXiv:2311.07745)** together with the surrounding ANPL "Simplified Belief-Dependent POMDP Planning" line — it already proves a regret/value bound for simplifying a POMDP component under a fixed reward, in continuous, high-dimensional (including image) observation spaces, which is structurally identical to what pillar 1 wants to prove for belief-hypothesis clustering. The genuinely open sliver is: (i) their equivalence is always w.r.t. one fixed reward/task, never a *family* of goals simultaneously, and (ii) they don't use an explicit per-hypothesis vectorized "decision signature" q_k(g) that is reused/compared across candidate hypotheses the way this proposal does — they bound scalar value/reward difference, not vector-ranking agreement. Whether that sliver is enough for a bound-level theoretical contribution (as opposed to an application-level one) is doubtful; it is very likely provable as a two-line corollary of either bisimulation or the ANPL machinery.

**Pillar 2 — amortized decision-regret VOI without nested inner planning.**
**Partially open, with two serious threats that jointly close most of the gap.** Deep Adaptive Design (Foster et al., ICML 2021, arXiv:2103.02438) already solves "amortize VOI without solving a nested design/planning problem per candidate observation" — it trains a network offline against a contrastive information-gain bound and deploys in milliseconds. Its gap relative to pillar 2 is that its VOI target is generic parameter/mutual information gain, not "would this observation flip the argmax action" (decision-regret VOI). Separately, "Value of Information and Reward Specification in Active Inference and POMDPs" (Ran Wei, arXiv:2408.06542) shows that EFE's epistemic term is *already* a non-nested, closed-form approximation to Bayes-optimal information value, and explicitly quantifies the optimality gap between EFE-VOI and true decision value — i.e., someone has already built and characterized a cheap, non-nested surrogate for decision-relevant VOI in belief-MDPs. **Strongest threat to pillar 2: Ran Wei, arXiv:2408.06542**, precisely because it operates in belief-MDP language (the proposal's own setting) and explicitly measures the gap between an amortized VOI surrogate and the "true" reward/decision value — which is exactly the theoretical object pillar 2 wants to introduce, just not phrased as "does the compressed decision-mode representation predict an argmax flip." The genuinely open piece is using the *compressed decision-mode* representation itself (M << K modes) as the substrate for a cheap "would this action-flip" classifier — that specific mechanism (cheap flip-prediction from a decision-clustered belief) does not appear to exist yet, but "amortized non-nested VOI" as a general idea is not new.

**Pillar 3 — goal-generalization of compression (preserve preferred actions across a family of goals, not a single reward).**
This is the **most genuinely open** of the three pillars, but only because almost no cited prior art bothers to test multi-goal generality at all — not because there is deep evidence it is hard or unexplored territory conceptually. Bisimulation, value-directed compression, ANPL's simplified-POMDP program, and Value Equivalence are all defined and proven for a *single, fixed* reward function; none of the retrieved papers states or proves a compression/regret bound that must hold simultaneously across a family of goals (e.g., all image-goal-conditioned rewards). Value Equivalence (Grimm et al., arXiv:2011.03506) comes conceptually closest because its equivalence is stated relative to an arbitrary *set* of (policy, value-function) pairs — which could in principle be instantiated as "one value function per goal in the family" — but the paper never does this, and doing so is a straightforward but real extension, not a solved problem. **Strongest threat to pillar 3: none of the surveyed papers is a direct threat**; the closest conceptual relative is Grimm et al.'s VE principle (arXiv:2011.03506), and goal-conditioned value functions / UVFAs (Schaul et al. 2015, not separately searched here but foundational and well known) establish that "Q(z,a,g)" objects are commodity, so the *representation* isn't new — only "compress hypotheses so the Q(z,·,g) ranking is preserved for every g in a family, with a bound" appears to be actually unclaimed.

## GO / STOP recommendation

**GO — conditional and narrowly scoped.** All three pillars sit inside well-populated prior art in their *general form* (equivalence-by-decision-quantity is Grimm et al. 2020; regret/value-preserving belief-component simplification is the ANPL Technion program; amortized non-nested VOI is Foster et al. DAD + Ran Wei's EFE analysis). None of the three pillars, as stated, is a wholly new theoretical object. However, two concrete, defensible differentiators survive and are not already claimed by any single retrieved paper:

1. **Multi-goal (family-of-goals) invariance of the compression, with a bound that must hold jointly across the family**, not per-fixed-reward. Every value/regret-preserving compression result found (bisimulation, ANPL simplification, value-directed compression, Value Equivalence) is proven/instantiated for one reward at a time. Proving (or even carefully demonstrating empirically) that M decision modes stay valid across a goal distribution — with a bound whose ε accounts for goal-family diameter — is not present in any single source above and is a legitimate, citable gap.
2. **Using the compressed decision-mode structure itself as the substrate for the amortized flip-prediction VOI classifier**, i.e., pillar 2's VOI network operates on the M-mode compressed representation rather than on raw K-particle beliefs or a generic mutual-information surrogate. DAD amortizes generic info-gain from full posteriors; Ran Wei's EFE analysis is a scalar closed-form surrogate, not a learned classifier over a clustered decision-mode representation. Making the amortized VOI cheap *because* it only has to reason over M << K modes (rather than K particles) is a concrete architectural novelty that combines pillars 1 and 2 in a way no single retrieved paper does.

Both differentiators require the paper to explicitly position pillar 1's bound as a **special case / straightforward corollary** of bisimulation-metric theory and the ANPL "Simplified POMDP" framework (cite Ferns et al., arXiv:2311.07745, arXiv:2310.01791 directly and concede the overlap), and to position pillar 2 as building on, not inventing, amortized VOI (cite Foster et al. arXiv:2103.02438 and Ran Wei arXiv:2408.06542 directly). If the paper instead tries to claim pillar 1's regret bound or pillar 2's "amortization without nested planning" as the headline novel contribution without these citations and the corollary framing, it will not survive review — a knowledgeable POMDP-theory or active-inference reviewer will flag both immediately. The defensible, GO-worthy framing is: *"decision-signature clustering across a goal family, with an amortized flip-predictor operating on the compressed modes"* — not *"we invented regret-bounded belief compression"* or *"we invented amortized VOI."*
