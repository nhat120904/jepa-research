\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
We study three questions: how the learned world-model target affects ranking on identical proposals, whether repeated selection improves closed-loop control, and how prediction/readout computation scales with candidates and queries. All main comparisons use deployable learned methods.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
\paragraph{Task and planner.}
PushT requires moving a planar T-shaped block into an image-specified pose. A frozen Diffusion Policy~\cite{chi2023diffusion} supplies $K=8$ action proposals per decision. We evaluate execution horizons $L=8$ and $L=15$, with a 300-step episode limit. Native success requires post-action coverage greater than .95 before the limit. For ranking, each method scores the same complete candidate banks. For control, each method replans after executing its chosen chunk; visited states then depend on earlier selections.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
P0 executes the first policy proposal. END is an endpoint world model: it predicts endpoint image features and proprioception, then uses a learned reader; its objective combines normalized feature MSE, score consistency, and weighted ranking. DIR directly scores $(C,\bA,g)$ using the same dense labels. DIR-large is a 14-layer, 11.31M-parameter direct scorer, compared with CTA's 10.95M, END's 11.14M, and DIR's 6.57M. These counts exclude the shared frozen image encoder and policy. The original CTA/DIR source groups have distinct weights but share an optimizer and global gradient clipping; DIR-large is trained independently. Full optimization and selection details are in the supplement.

The official DINO-WM checkpoint~\cite{zhou2025dinowm} provides a spatial-feature world-model reference on the same proposals. It retains its own image encoding, velocity inputs, training, and latent-distance cost; our arms use PCA features and agent-position history. This external comparison therefore matches candidate banks and horizons, while the CTA/END/DIR comparisons more closely match observation and training interfaces. DIR-large is evaluated on fixed banks; a completed closed-loop run is not available for this arm.

\paragraph{Evaluation population and uncertainty.}
The principal ranking population comprises 5,558 logged P0 decisions from 200 development initializations (roots 2200--2399), of which 3,433 have nonconstant geometry labels. Training and checkpoint-selection banks are separate, but these evaluation roots have been reused during development. Each main model has one training seed. We report 95\% root-cluster bootstrap intervals and paired contrasts, rather than treating decisions as independent samples. Historical control uses the old batched proposal sampler, whose batch-group sensitivity is documented in the supplement. Independent multi-seed control under the corrected sampling protocol remains necessary for a submission-strength benchmark.

\paragraph{Ranking metric.}
For bank $b$, utility $y_{bk}$, and selected candidate $\hat k_b$, geometry retained gap is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{RG}=\frac{\sum_b(y_{b\hat k_b}-y_{b0})}
{\sum_b(\max_k y_{bk}-y_{b0})}.
\label{eq:rg}
\end{equation}
It measures aggregate improvement over candidate zero relative to the available bank improvement. It is not success probability or retained information; it can be negative and is undefined at zero denominator. Ties use the smallest candidate index. Bootstrap estimates aggregate numerator and denominator after resampling roots and use identical resamples for paired comparisons.

\subsection{World-model targets on fixed proposals}
\label{sec:ranking}
\Cref{fig:ranking} compares deployable methods on identical $L=8$ banks. CTA reaches RG .680, versus .635 for endpoint prediction and .483 for direct scoring. The paired CTA--END difference is +.045 [.011,.079], and CTA--DIR is +.197 [.153,.241] (\cref{tab:pairedrank}). Thus the conditional segment target improves this development ranking measure relative to the tested endpoint and direct alternatives.

\paragraph{Capacity control.}
Increasing the direct scorer to 11.31M parameters yields RG .502 [.445,.553], still below CTA by +.178 [.138,.220]. The direct model's own improvement is +.019 [$-.021,.058$]. The observed CTA advantage is therefore not explained by this parameter increase. The control does not isolate conditional encoding, future supervision, temporal sampling, or factorization individually; those require separate matched ablations. In particular, a goal-independent direct feature extractor followed by a reader would test how much of the gain comes from the prediction target rather than the two-stage interface.

\subsection{Closed-loop development control}
\label{sec:closed}
\Cref{tab:closed} reports success and normalized maximum coverage for 200 common initial roots. CTA achieves 70.0\% success at $L=8$ and 60.5\% at $L=15$, improving over the proposal policy by nine percentage points at both horizons. Paired 95\% intervals for CTA--P0 are [1,17.5] and [1,17] points, respectively. These results show that the learned forecast can guide repeated action selection in this development setting.

The differences between learned methods are less conclusive. At $L=8$, CTA--END is +1 point [$-7.5,9.5$], CTA--DIR +2.5 [$-5.5,10.5$], and CTA--DINO-WM +2 [$-6,10$]. At $L=15$, these differences are +6.5 [$-2.5,15.5$], +9 [.5,17.5], and +4.5 [$-4.5,13.5$]. The exact McNemar test for CTA--DIR at $L=15$ gives $p=.05045$, so that comparison should not be described as an established significance result. No correction for multiple exploratory comparisons is applied. Mean-score contrast intervals cross zero. Ranking gains on fixed banks therefore do not establish a general closed-loop advantage over learned world-model alternatives.

\subsection{Candidate and query scaling}
\label{sec:compute}
\paragraph{Ranking with more candidates.}
A separate logged-bank experiment uses 2,810 P0 decisions from 100 development roots and scores banks with $K\in\{8,16,32,64\}$. These are newly logged banks rather than exact prefixes of the earlier $K=8$ run. Define mean geometry gain as $\Delta_K=|\mathcal B|^{-1}\sum_b(y_{b\hat k_b}-y_{b0})$. CTA's gain rises from $.608\times10^{-3}$ at $K=8$ to $.999\times10^{-3}$ at $K=64$ (\cref{fig:scaling}a); the saved paired increase is $.391\times10^{-3}$, with 95\% root-bootstrap interval $[.328,.458]\times10^{-3}$. Retained gap remains approximately .62. At $K=64$, CTA--END gain is $.110\times10^{-3}$ $[.064,.155]\times10^{-3}$ and CTA--DIR-large is $.260\times10^{-3}$ $[.172,.347]\times10^{-3}$. This checks the ranking advantage under an expanded proposal workload, without treating it as a closed-loop or independent-test result.

\paragraph{Prediction and readout cost.}
\Cref{fig:scaling}b,c measure the predictor/readout components for $K\in\{8,16,64\}$ and $G\in\{1,16\}$. Measurements use synchronized CUDA execution on an H100 3g.40gb MIG instance, ten initial states, three warmups, and ten repeats per state. All three curves exclude the same shared context encoding, proposal sampling, and simulator execution. Repeated queries reuse the CTA and END predictions; DIR evaluates its joint scorer again. The sixteen queries are image views of one PushT target pose, rather than sixteen distinct semantic goals.

At $K=8$, CTA takes 4.11ms for one query and 16.87ms for sixteen, compared with END's 4.52/23.40ms and DIR's 2.54/26.81ms. Direct scoring is cheaper for one query at this candidate count. With sixteen queries, CTA reduces scorer time by 27.9\% relative to END and 37.1\% relative to DIR. At $K=64$, CTA takes 12.96/114.42ms, versus END's 20.09/171.04ms and DIR's 13.94/206.67ms. The benefit depends on both the candidate batch and the repeated-readout workload.



The shared context path takes approximately 6.5ms, while the common diffusion proposal sampler takes 765ms at $K=8$ and 923ms at $K=64$. Proposal generation dominates this implementation, so component savings do not imply a comparable reduction in total control latency. DINO-WM's separately measured encoding/rollout/cost path uses a different timing scope and precision; it is reported in the supplement rather than mixed into the matched component curves. End-to-end latency and peak memory remain to be benchmarked under a common precision and input protocol.
