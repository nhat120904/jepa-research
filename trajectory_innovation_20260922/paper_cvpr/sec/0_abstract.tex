\begin{abstract}
Visual world models predict action-conditioned changes in observations or latent features. Their prediction target determines what the dynamics model must learn and what information is available to a planner. We investigate a target learned for a complete action segment: \emph{Conditional Trajectory Abstraction} (CTA). A training-only encoder maps observed context and sampled future observations to 16 quantized tokens. A goal-conditioned reader and a future-feature decoder jointly supervise this target. The world model then learns to forecast its token distributions from context and a proposed action chunk, using categorical prediction and reader-score consistency. At deployment, the reader evaluates the predicted coordinate means without receiving candidate actions; a forecast can be reused across goal queries. This yields a 48-scalar segment interface while retaining a separately learned model of action consequences. On identical eight-candidate PushT development banks, CTA recovers 0.680 of the available geometry improvement, versus 0.635 for endpoint prediction and 0.502 for a parameter-matched direct scorer. In 200 development episodes it achieves 70.0\% success, compared with 61.0\% for the proposal policy and 69.0\% for endpoint prediction. Component benchmarks characterize scaling with candidates and goal views. The results support learned segment targets for visual world modeling; independent multi-seed control and semantic-goal transfer remain to be established.
\end{abstract}
