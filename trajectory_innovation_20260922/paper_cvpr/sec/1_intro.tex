\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Visual world models learn how an environment changes under an agent's actions, enabling a planner to evaluate predicted outcomes before execution~\cite{hafner2019planet,zhou2025dinowm,assran2025vjepa2}. A central design choice is the \emph{prediction target}. Pixel reconstruction, spatial visual features, compact tokens, and reward-oriented latent states preserve different information and impose different computational demands. Understanding this choice matters both for learning dynamics and for using those dynamics in decisions.

Recent visual world models build on strong pretrained encoders. DINO-WM forecasts DINOv2 patch features~\cite{zhou2025dinowm,oquab2024dinov2}, and V-JEPA~2-AC forecasts features of a pretrained video encoder~\cite{assran2025vjepa2}. Such representations provide broad visual supervision and support image-goal planning. CompACT reduces the spatial representation to a small set of discrete observation tokens~\cite{kim2026compact}. These advances leave another design dimension open: the temporal unit and supervision of the prediction target. Instead of forecasting a sequence of independently represented observations, can a world model forecast one learned description of an action segment, conditioned on the context already available to its readout?

We investigate this question through \emph{Conditional Trajectory Abstraction} (CTA). During training, a target encoder observes context $C$ and the actual future segment $\tau$ of an action chunk, then produces a compact code $S$. A goal-conditioned reader learns to evaluate $(C,S,g)$, while a feature decoder reconstructs sampled future features from $(C,S)$. The world model $p_\theta(S\mid C,\bA)$ forecasts this target from context and proposed actions. Thus the predictive object is learned from action consequences rather than fixed to the visual encoder's output. The reader never receives $\bA$: candidate-specific information enters through the world model's forecast. Actual future observations are used only in training.

Conditioning the source and readout on the same observed context follows the idea of contextual coding~\cite{li2021dcvc,micheli2024deltairis}. Representing a multi-step segment also connects to compact trajectory models~\cite{jiang2023tap}. CTA brings these ideas to action-conditioned visual prediction, with a target shaped by both future-feature supervision and a learned decision readout. This differs from searching for actions in a trajectory-code space, and from compressing each observation before an autoregressive rollout. It also differs from direct value guidance~\cite{nakamoto2024vgps}: direct scoring is a valid alternative that need not predict an explicit future target. Whether future supervision helps is a comparison to measure, rather than an assumption of the method.

CTA predicts all segment-code coordinates in parallel. The code encoder and world model receive no goal input, so predicted codes can be cached while a reader evaluates different goal queries: \emph{predict once, query many}. This input separation is shared with other outcome-based planning systems~\cite{qi2025gpc,cui2026dreamsteer}; our focus is the learned conditional segment target within that separation. The present PushT experiments use multiple image views of one target pose and do not evaluate transfer to unseen semantic goals.

We evaluate world-model targets through a common proposal-based planner. A frozen diffusion policy proposes candidate chunks, and each learned method selects among them. Offline comparisons hold states and complete action banks fixed; closed-loop comparisons measure the consequences of repeated selection. On eight-candidate development banks, CTA improves geometry ranking over endpoint prediction and both ordinary and parameter-matched direct scorers. In closed-loop development it improves success over the proposal policy, while learned alternatives remain close and their differences uncertain. Timing measurements expose how predictor/readout cost scales with candidate count and repeated queries.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt,itemsep=1pt]
\item A conditional segment representation for visual world modeling, learned from observed futures and shared context rather than prescribed as a sequence of frame features.
\item A world-model training architecture combining a quantized future target, a goal-conditioned reader, auxiliary feature reconstruction, and forecast-to-reader consistency. Its deployment readout has no access to proposed actions or observed futures.
\item A comparison of learned prediction targets under shared proposal banks, including a capacity-matched direct control, closed-loop development results, and candidate/query scaling measurements.
\end{itemize}
