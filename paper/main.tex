\begin{abstract}
World-model planners do not consume average representation or decoding error;
they optimize the ordering induced by a terminal cost on their own candidate
distribution. We introduce an optimizer-conditioned cost audit that separates
dynamics error, candidate availability, immediate selection, and adaptive
search. On two contact-rich MetaWorld tasks and two released visual world-model
checkpoints, every candidate is executed from a simulator snapshot and its
endpoint is re-encoded, making candidate dynamics exact. With the same CEM
budget, a simulator-state reference cost solves push in $64/64$ episodes and
pick-place in $49/64$, whereas latent $L_2$ solves $0/64$ in every cell.
A supervised state-decoding cost reaches at most $5/64$ despite a
$2.1$\,cm median error on its object channel on held-out expert frames.
The audit separates two failure modes. Latent $L_2$ carries only weak and
task-dependent ordering that becomes anti-aligned under search: its rank
correlation with the physical reference is $0.25$--$0.27$ on the two push cells
but indistinguishable from zero on both pick-place cells, and it is negative in
all four cells after refitting. Stateprobe carries substantially more initial
ordering ($\rho=0.43$--$0.55$) and loses it under search, falling to
$0.11$--$0.16$ after refitting; on identical candidates its selection incurs
structured regret beyond a residual-matched noisy-score null, and changing only
the refitting cost then causes later populations to lose better physical
candidates. Exact-success candidates are also sparse in the realized final
populations; a matched-snapshot control finds a limited within-snapshot
refitting effect, leaving an additional state-distribution effect that our
design does not quantify.
Apparently accurate per-channel decoding can therefore coexist with a cost that
becomes unreliable on optimizer-induced inputs. The causal selection and
refitting diagnosis is established for the fixed stateprobe composition.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Visual world models increasingly support planning by predicting future latent
states and minimizing their distance to a goal
\citep{zhou2024dinowm,jepawms2025}. This decomposition creates at least three
distinct failure sites: the predictor may produce the wrong future, the search
may not propose a useful action sequence, or the terminal cost may prefer a
physically worse sequence. Aggregate prediction or decoding error does not
distinguish these cases. A planner consumes an \emph{ordering} over its own
candidates, often after the optimizer has moved those candidates away from the
data on which the representation or readout was validated.

We ask: \emph{if every candidate future is exact, when does a
representation-derived cost still fail to support contact-rich planning?} In
MetaWorld~\citep{yu2020metaworld}, we snapshot the simulator, execute each CEM
candidate, render its endpoint, and pass that observation through the frozen
encoder. This exact-dynamics construction preserves the real representation
and terminal scorer while removing the learned predictor. Simulator state
provides a reference score for every candidate.

We call the resulting procedure an \emph{optimizer-conditioned cost audit}.
It scores identical populations with both the deployed proxy and a physical
reference, compares real selection with a matched-noise permutation null, and
intervenes on the cost used for adaptive CEM refitting. A separate availability
test asks whether an exactly successful sequence is present in the realized
final population. The audit separates:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item whether failure persists without learned-dynamics error;
  \item whether the finite planner budget can solve the task with an
  informative cost;
  \item whether useful candidates are present under the realized search; and
  \item whether the proxy selects and preserves the better candidates that are
  available.
\end{enumerate}

We contribute both the reusable audit and a controlled empirical diagnosis.
First, exact dynamics plus latent or probe-derived cost remains near failure,
while the same search budget succeeds with a simulator-state cost. Second,
applying the audit to both costs separates two failure modes that the success
table alone conflates. Latent $L_2$ fails by \emph{weak, task-dependent initial
ordering that becomes anti-aligned under search}: on pick-place its ranking of
contact candidates is already indistinguishable from independent of the physical
reference, recovering the physically best elites at or below chance before any
refitting, whereas on push it retains a weak but nonzero initial ordering that
turns negative only after refitting.
Stateprobe fails by
\emph{eroded and exploited ordering}: per-channel decoding that appears accurate
on held-out expert observations does supply real initial ordering, but rank
correlation and elite recall collapse during refitting, the proxy misranks
identical candidates beyond an equally noisy residual-matched baseline, and a
cost-only intervention shows that this misranking degrades later proposals.
Third, we measure exact-success candidate availability rather than silently
attributing every failure to ranking, and use a matched-snapshot branch control
to bound how much of the observed sparsity the refitting cost itself explains
within a snapshot.
The causal selection and refitting tests are run for stateprobe; latent $L_2$ is
covered by the exact-dynamics terminal-cost comparison and the
same-population ranking audit.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Latent world-model planning.}
DINO-WM plans in the frozen feature space of a self-supervised visual encoder
\citep{zhou2024dinowm}. A subsequent TMLR study systematically evaluates design
choices for joint-embedding predictive world models and releases the
MetaWorld checkpoints and planning stack used here
\citep{jepawms2025}. Our experiments audit two of those released checkpoints;
they do not make a claim about all JEPA systems. More broadly, a
decision-centric view of world-model evaluation argues that predictive quality
should be connected to downstream decision quality
\citep{yu2026wmeval}. We instantiate that connection at the optimizer--cost
interface.

\paragraph{Decision-aware model and cost learning.}
That predictive accuracy is the wrong target for a model used inside a planner
is a long-standing position. Value-aware model learning weights model error by
its effect on the value backup rather than by observation
likelihood~\citep{farahmand2017vaml,farahmand2018itervaml}; the value
equivalence principle characterizes when different models are
interchangeable for planning~\citep{grimm2020ve}; MuZero learns a model
supervised only by decision-relevant quantities~\citep{schrittwieser2020muzero};
and control-oriented model learning differentiates through the planner
itself~\citep{nikishin2022omd}. That literature reweights the \emph{dynamics}
objective by decision relevance, and its notion of relevance is a value
function on the model's own state space. Our setting is the complementary one:
we remove learned-dynamics error by construction and isolate the terminal-cost
interface under a fixed search process, evaluating it on the distribution that
the optimizer itself generates. Proposal generation and the optimizer remain
active parts of the system, and the availability measurements below address
them directly. The audit can be read as measuring planner-facing ranking
agreement between a released latent cost and a physical reference on precisely
that distribution.

\paragraph{Optimization against imperfect models and costs.}
Model-based control has long studied policies that exploit learned-dynamics
error~\citep{janner2019trust} and the mismatch between prediction objectives
and control performance~\citep{lambert2020objective}. More recent work shows
that gradient-based planning can produce out-of-distribution or adversarial
action trajectories and smooths the induced planning landscape through
adversarial training~\citep{parthasarathy2025traintest}. We instead hold
dynamics exact and study the terminal scorer. Selection over noisy estimates
already creates an optimizer's curse~\citep{smith2006optimizer}; proxy and
target can additionally decouple under optimization
\citep{gao2023overopt,skalse2022defining,karwowski2024goodhart}. Our
matched-residual null measures how much candidate misranking remains beyond
the generic noisy-selection effect.

\paragraph{Closest concurrent diagnoses.}
Concurrent work in latent planning addresses complementary interfaces.
RC-aux reshapes a representation using a reachability objective
\citep{rcaux2026}; TRM learns a trajectory-ordering terminal metric
\citep{trm2026}; and ACID augments the terminal objective with
inverse-dynamics consistency along a proposed trajectory
\citep{acid2026}. IMWM replaces learned dynamics by environment rollout and
identifies finite-population proposal coverage as an important failure mode
\citep{imwm2026}. These studies establish related failure modes, but leave open
how to separate cost selection from candidate availability under a controlled
optimizer intervention. Our audit provides that decomposition: exact dynamics,
dual scoring of identical contact-rich candidates, a residual-matched null, a
cost-only intervention on adaptive refitting, and explicit measurement of
exact-success availability in one harness.

\subsection{Models, tasks, and planning protocol}

We evaluate the released \texttt{dino\_wm\_metaworld} and
\texttt{jepa\_wm\_metaworld} checkpoints through their public
\texttt{EncPredWM} interface~\citep{jepawms2025}. Both use their released
preprocessing and action normalization. The contact tasks are
\texttt{mw-push} and \texttt{mw-pick-place}; \texttt{mw-reach} is used only as
a free-space control. The goal image is the scripted expert trajectory's final
frame. Episode success is the simulator's task flag at the final environment
step, not an any-step latch. Its object-to-goal distance radii are $5$\,cm for
push and $7$\,cm for pick-place.

Unless otherwise stated, the mechanistic experiments use receding-horizon CEM
\citep{rubinstein1997cem} with 100 candidates, a top-10\% elite set, six
refitting iterations, and action horizon $H=6$. We denote this budget by
$100\times6$. The simulator-state reference arm is a positive control for the
planner and budget; it is not a deployable vision-only method.

\subsection{Oracle dynamics and three terminal costs}

At each replan, the oracle-dynamics harness saves the full MuJoCo state. For
every action sequence $a$, it restores the same snapshot, executes $a$ in the
simulator, renders the terminal observation, and encodes that observation with
the frozen visual encoder. Thus candidate transitions are exact, while the
representation and terminal scorer remain unchanged.

We compare three costs:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Latent $L_2$}, the released Euclidean distance between the
  encoded endpoint and goal.
  \item \textbf{Stateprobe}, an interpretable privileged diagnostic that decodes
  object position $\hat o$ and end-effector position $\hat h$ with
  supervised probes on frozen
  endpoint tokens using simulator labels, then applies the planning cost
  \begin{equation}
    \hat c(a)=\|\hat o_T(a)-\hat o_g\|_2+
       0.5\|\hat h_T(a)-\hat o_T(a)\|_2.
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Simulator-state reference}, which applies the same form to
  simulator object and hand state:
  \begin{equation}
    c^*(a)=\|o_T(a)-o_g\|_2+
       0.5\|h_T(a)-o_T(a)\|_2.
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
The reference is a shaped physical cost, not task return or a claim of a
unique ``true'' objective.

\subsection{Selection, null, refitting, and availability estimands}

For a population $\mathcal{A}$ scored by stateprobe and simulator reference, we
define within-population selection regret
\begin{equation}
 R_{\mathrm{sel}} =
 c^*\!\left(\arg\min_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\hat c(a)\right)
 - \min_{a\in\mathcal{A}} c^*(a).
\end{equation}
It measures the opportunity lost by proxy selection within the sampled
population.
Optimism is $o(a)=c^*(a)-\hat c(a)$. We report optimism enrichment of the
proxy-selected elite set relative to its population. Let
$e_o(a)=\|\hat o_T(a)-o_T(a)\|_2$ and let
$E_{\hat c}\subset\mathcal A$ be the 10 candidates with smallest stateprobe
cost. The object-only elite error excess and optimism enrichment are
\begin{align}
 \Delta_{\mathrm{err}}
 &= \frac{1}{|E_{\hat c}|}\sum_{a\in E_{\hat c}}e_o(a)
    -\frac{1}{|\mathcal A|}\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}e_o(a),\\
 \Delta_{\mathrm{opt}}
 &= \frac{1}{|E_{\hat c}|}\sum_{a\in E_{\hat c}}o(a)
    -\frac{1}{|\mathcal A|}\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}o(a).
\end{align}
The first diagnostic concerns object decoding only; hand error enters the
composite stateprobe cost and is reported separately in
Appendix~\ref{app:probe}.

The matched-residual null writes $\hat c(a)=c^*(a)+r(a)$ and randomly permutes
the observed residual multiset within each population before selecting an
argmin. It preserves candidate states, reference costs, marginal error
magnitudes, and search budget while breaking residual--candidate association.
We use 1,000 permutations.

To test adaptive refitting, we fork CEM from candidate-for-candidate identical
first populations. One branch refits on $\hat c$ and the other on $c^*$.
Subsequent samples use the same base Gaussian noise transformed by each
branch's own fitted mean and variance. A true-state \emph{carrier} supplies the
shared sequence of receding-horizon simulator snapshots. The contrast is
therefore a within-snapshot intervention on search.

Exact-success candidate availability is the fraction of replans whose
final-iteration population contains at least one sequence satisfying the exact
MetaWorld success predicate at its horizon endpoint. Selected success asks
whether the proxy argmin is such a sequence. This is deliberately not called
independent proposal coverage because the population is measured after earlier
proxy-guided refits.

For the two contact tasks, physical task distance is the 3-D Euclidean
object-to-goal distance
$d_{\mathrm{task}}(a)=\|o_T(a)-o_g\|_2$. Its within-population regret is
\begin{equation}
 R_{\mathrm{phys}} =
 d_{\mathrm{task}}\!\left(\arg\min_{a\in\mathcal A}\hat c(a)\right)
 - \min_{a\in\mathcal A}d_{\mathrm{task}}(a).
\end{equation}
This differs from $R_{\mathrm{sel}}$, which evaluates the composite shaped
reference cost $c^*$.

\subsection{Evaluation splits and uncertainty}

The terminal-cost comparison uses fresh paired environment seeds
30000--30063. The
same-population audit and matched null use independently fixed seeds
41000--41015; the branch intervention uses 42000--42007; and the availability audit
uses 40000--40015. Confidence intervals for population-level estimands are
percentile bootstraps clustered by environment seed (5,000 resamples). Episode
rates use Wilson intervals, and paired episode contrasts use paired resampling
or exact McNemar tests as appropriate. These reported evaluation ranges are
disjoint from the eight off-policy probe-training environment seeds
20000--20007. Uncertainty is over environment seeds, not
checkpoint-training seeds.

\paragraph{Implementation integrity.}
We corrected three environment-side mismatches before evaluation: renderer
construction relative to camera assignment, the vertical image flip used in
training preprocessing, and goal-frame selection. With the shipped latent
$L_2$ planner and its larger upstream budget, our Reach control succeeds in
$6/16$ episodes ($37.5\%$, Wilson 95\% interval $[18.5,61.4]\%$), compatible
with the reported $44.8\pm8.9\%$ but not a seed-matched replication
\citep{jepawms2025}. The exact audit protocol is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:audit}.

\subsection{Held-out decoding does not predict ranking under search}

Table~\ref{tab:probeexpertmain} first evaluates the exact probes used by the
planner on a held-out expert-trajectory split. Object localization appears
strong in isolation: median Euclidean error is $2.1$\,cm, below both task
distance radii. The hand readout is markedly weaker, at $5.6$--$6.7$\,cm median
error.

We state the scope of this validation precisely, because it is the quantity a
practitioner would be most tempted to over-read. The held-out split validates
the two decoded \emph{channels} separately. It does not evaluate the deployed
scalar cost $\hat c$ as a whole: that cost composes a decoded endpoint object, a
decoded goal-frame object, and a decoded hand position through two norms, and we
did not measure its error against $c^*$ on the held-out expert split. The
$2.1$\,cm object-channel figure therefore neither estimates nor bounds the error
of the deployed scalar cost: the channels enter through two norms, and their
errors may cancel or compound. It should be read as ``one channel of this cost
looks accurate by the usual standard,'' which is exactly the kind of partial
evidence that motivates the audit, and not as a certificate that the cost is
accurate. A direct held-out evaluation of $\hat c$ remains an open validation
item (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

With that scope fixed, the planning result is still the informative one: a
readout whose object channel achieves apparently useful accuracy on ordinary
held-out frames supports at most $5/64$ successful plans.

\subsection{The stateprobe cost misranks identical candidates}\label{sec:selection}

We next inspect the iteration-0 population at each audited replan, before
adaptive refitting at that snapshot. Each of the same 100 action sequences is
executed in MuJoCo and scored by both $\hat c$ and $c^*$.
Table~\ref{tab:selection} reports the selection diagnostics and matched null.
First, candidates in the proxy top-10\% have $1.24$--$1.40$\,cm more object
decode error than the full population. Second, proxy elites have
$2.16$--$2.33$\,cm more optimistic residuals than their populations, with all
seed-clustered confidence intervals above zero. Third, proxy argmins incur
$2.05$--$2.48$\,cm reference-cost regret within the sampled set---a substantial
loss on tasks with $5$\,cm and $7$\,cm distance radii.

\subsection{Latent $L_2$ fails differently: weak, task-dependent ordering that
turns anti-aligned}\label{sec:l2audit}

The same-population estimands apply to any terminal cost, so we run the ranking
audit for the deployed latent $L_2$ cost as well. Two of the four estimands
transfer directly. Spearman $\rho$ between the proxy and the reference is
rank-based and therefore scale-free, and $R_{\mathrm{sel}}$ is defined purely in
reference units. We do not report $\Delta_{\mathrm{err}}$ or
$\Delta_{\mathrm{opt}}$ for latent $L_2$: optimism $c^*-\hat c$ is not
dimensionally meaningful for a cost that is not expressed in metres, and object
decode error is a property of the stateprobe readout that $L_2$ never consults.
Each cost is audited on the populations its own search produces; iteration-0
populations are drawn from the same fixed initial distribution but are not
candidate-identical across costs, whereas within an arm the proxy and reference

and loses it under search, which is what makes the optimizer-conditioned
mechanism in Sections~\ref{sec:selection} and~\ref{sec:branch} the relevant
explanation for it. On pick-place, latent $L_2$ needs no such explanation: it
does not order contact candidates by physical quality even on fresh populations
drawn from the untouched initial distribution, so its $0/64$ there is already
accounted for before the optimizer acts. On push the picture is genuinely
unresolved. A weak but nonzero initial ordering is present and becomes negative
after refitting, which is compatible with search concentrating on a region that
$L_2$ prefers and physics does not; but we do not run the refitting intervention
for $L_2$, so we describe the anti-alignment rather than attributing it to
refitting. Whether adaptive search causally contributes to the push failure of
$L_2$ is left open.

A methodological caution applies to $R_{\mathrm{sel}}$ across stages. Within
either cost, regret falls between iterations 0 and 5 (for example $2.98$ to
$1.60$\,cm for DINO push $L_2$) because refitting concentrates the population
and compresses the spread of $c^*$ within it. Regret is comparable across costs
at a fixed iteration, not across iterations.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Mechanistic picture.}
The two costs fail the same benchmark for different reasons, and the audit is
what separates them. Latent $L_2$ ranks contact candidates weakly or not at all
by physical quality on fresh initial populations, and the two tasks differ: on
pick-place its ordering is already indistinguishable from independent, with
reference elite recall at or below chance before any refitting, so the failure
is evident without invoking the optimizer; on push a weak initial ordering
survives and turns negative only after refitting, and because we did not run the
refitting intervention for $L_2$ we cannot say whether adaptive search causally
contributes there. The stateprobe is the case that motivates the title: its
object channel is accurate on held-out expert frames, the composite cost does
carry real ordering on fresh populations, and that ordering then degrades where
it is used. Immediate
misranking exceeds the matched-noise baseline, and the refitting intervention
shows that it changes which physical candidates remain available. At the same
time, exact-success candidates are sparse in final populations, with the
matched-snapshot control showing a limited within-snapshot refitting
contribution and leaving an unquantified state-distribution contribution
alongside it. The resulting diagnosis is a coupled
selection-and-availability failure at the
representation--readout--cost--optimizer interface, holding for a cost that has
ordering to lose.

\paragraph{Implications.}
A planning representation should be evaluated for the ordering it supports on
the optimizer-induced distribution. When simulator state is available, we
recommend the audit used here: dual-score identical populations, report rank
agreement and within-population regret, compare against a residual-matched
baseline, measure exact-success availability with its horizon and snapshot
conditioning, and intervene on adaptive refitting. This protocol distinguishes
whether method development should prioritize cost learning, proposal
generation, or both.

\paragraph{Scope and limitations.}
The audit covers MetaWorld, two contact tasks, two released checkpoints, one
fixed stateprobe architecture and training seed, and CEM. The branch
intervention uses eight seeds and a shared true-state snapshot carrier;
uncertainty is across environment seeds rather than checkpoint-training seeds.
The simulator-state cost and probe supervision are privileged diagnostics, not
deployable methods. The mechanism tests establish misranking for the complete
stateprobe composition---representation, readout, cost form, and
optimizer---rather than for the encoder alone. The
same-population regret is local to sampled candidates, and final availability
also depends on previous refits, horizon, and encountered snapshots. Planner
endpoint controls include MPPI and random shooting, but the causal mechanism
has only been tested for CEM. Physical-robot, probe/weight sensitivity, and
independent initial-proposal studies remain open.

Three specific gaps are worth naming because they bound what the current
evidence supports. First, the deployed stateprobe scalar cost has not been
evaluated end-to-end on the held-out expert split; only its two decoded
channels have. Second, latent $L_2$ is covered by the terminal-cost comparison
and the same-population ranking audit of Section~\ref{sec:l2audit}, but not by
the refitting intervention, so its negative final-population rank correlation is
described rather than causally attributed. Third, the matched-residual null
establishes structured misranking at the level of within-population selection
regret. We have not run the corresponding closed-loop control, in which a
reference cost is perturbed by unstructured noise of matched magnitude and
planned with end to end. That control would separate how much of the
$5/64$ endpoint result is due to error magnitude and how much to error
structure, and we do not claim that separation here.

\section{Conclusion}

Exact candidate dynamics do not rescue the two tested representation-derived
costs, although the same finite search succeeds with an informative physical
reference. Auditing the two costs on identical populations shows that they fail
for different reasons. Latent $L_2$ orders contact candidates weakly at best
before refitting and anti-alignedly after it; on pick-place the failure is
already evident before refitting, while on push whether adaptive refitting
causally contributes remains unresolved. The stateprobe does supply real initial
ordering and then loses it:
optimizer-induced candidates erode rank agreement, structured residuals alter
immediate selection beyond a matched-noise null, and cost-only refitting changes
later candidate quality. Sparse exact-success availability compounds this loss,
with a limited within-snapshot refitting contribution and a further
state-distribution contribution that our design does not quantify.
World-model evaluation should therefore test a cost on the
distribution and decisions induced by its optimizer, and should report which of
these two regimes a given cost is in; held-out prediction or per-channel
decoding accuracy alone does not certify plannability.

\subsubsection*{Broader Impact Statement}
This work studies failure modes of simulation-based robot world-model planning
and may support more reliable evaluation of learned planning systems. The
experiments do not establish real-world robot safety or generality beyond the
evaluated tasks, checkpoints, costs, and planners. Overgeneralizing the
findings could create misplaced confidence in an audit or unwarranted
conclusions about representation learning more broadly. Independent simulator
and physical-system validation, together with deployment-specific safety
controls, remains necessary before applying these conclusions to robots.

\paragraph{Candidate execution.}
For every candidate action sequence, the harness restores an identical MuJoCo
snapshot, executes the sequence, renders the endpoint, and runs the released
encoder on that rendered observation. Simulator state is used only for
evaluation, stateprobe supervision, or the explicitly privileged reference
branch. Candidates are never assigned reference state by the learned
predictor.

\paragraph{Immediate selection.}
The first-population audit evaluates 100 sequences per seed before any CEM
refit. The top-10\% elite sets are independently formed under stateprobe and
reference cost. The residual null performs 1,000 within-population
permutations. Seed-clustered percentile intervals use 5,000 bootstrap
resamples.

\paragraph{Adaptive branch.}
Both CEM branches share the simulator snapshot, first candidate population,
and base Gaussian noise. Each branch transforms later common noise by its own
mean and variance. The reference-cost carrier determines the common sequence
of MPC snapshots, so the audit isolates search behavior at those snapshots
rather than comparing two closed-loop policies.

\paragraph{Candidate availability.}
Candidate-horizon success is evaluated with the simulator's exact task
predicate. It need not imply episode-end success after subsequent MPC replans.
Intervals cluster replans by their environment seed. Because this is measured
on final proxy-guided populations, it is not an independent estimate of the
initial proposal distribution's coverage.
