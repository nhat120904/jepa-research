\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Latent world models are increasingly used for visual robot planning: an encoder
maps observations to a latent, an action-conditioned predictor rolls that latent
forward, and a sampling optimiser searches for the action sequence minimising a
terminal cost against a goal latent. This proposal reviews the literature that a
newcomer must read to work in this area, and reports what a completed
diagnostic programme has established within it. The central empirical finding
is negative and specific: on two released MetaWorld checkpoints, when every
candidate future is made \emph{exact} by executing it in the simulator, a
privileged simulator-state cost solves push in $64/64$ episodes while the
released latent $L_2$ cost solves $0/64$, and a supervised state-decoding cost
reaches at most $5/64$ despite $1.64$--$1.71$\,cm held-out decoding error. The
defect is therefore not the predictor. Its mechanism appears only under search:
agreement between the decoding cost and the physical reference falls from
Spearman $0.43$--$0.55$ on initial candidate populations to $0.11$--$0.16$ after
the optimiser refits. A ladder of repairs---better readouts, relearned adapters,
encoder fine-tuning, ensemble pessimism, search-free control, and a
selection-aware encoder objective---each improved its own metric and moved
contact planning not at all.

This document is written to be argued with. Section~\ref{sec:lit} is the bulk:
it organises roughly fifty papers along four failure surfaces, tags each by the
evidentiary weight it can bear, and marks where the literature has and has not
settled a question. Section~\ref{sec:open} converts the surviving gaps into six
falsifiable directions, each stated with the evidence that keeps it alive, the
evidence that already constrains it, and the result that would end it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and problem statement}\label{sec:intro}
% ===============================================================

A visual world-model planner is a composition of four blocks. An encoder
$E$ maps an observation to a latent $z$. An action-conditioned predictor
$f$ maps $(z_t, a_t)$ to $z_{t+1}$. A proposal mechanism generates candidate
action sequences. A terminal cost $c(z_H, z_g)$ scores each candidate against a
goal, and the optimiser returns the minimiser. Success requires all four to
work; failure of any one produces the same observable, a task that does not get
solved.

This creates at least three distinct failure sites, and aggregate metrics cannot
separate them. The predictor may produce the wrong future. The proposal
mechanism may never generate a good candidate. The cost may prefer a physically
worse sequence over a better one that is present in the population. Reporting
prediction error, or decoding accuracy, or episode success rate, leaves the
attribution undetermined. Worse, a planner does not consume average error at
all: it consumes an \emph{ordering} over its own candidates, and by the time it
has refit its sampling distribution several times, those candidates are no
longer drawn from the distribution on which the representation or the readout
was validated.

The research programme reviewed here asks a narrow version of the question:
\emph{if every candidate future is exact, when does a representation-derived
cost still fail to support contact-rich planning?} It answers that question for
two released MetaWorld checkpoints by replacing the learned predictor with
literal simulator rollout, holding the encoder, the cost family, the optimiser
and the budget fixed. Section~\ref{sec:evidence} reports the result.

\paragraph{Purpose of this document}
This is a literature-weighted proposal with three jobs, in descending order of
page budget. First, to be the reading map for the area: what to read, in what
order, and---the part usually left implicit---\emph{with how much trust}.
Second, to report what the completed work established, only to the depth needed
to say which doors it closes. Third, to expose the remaining seams precisely
enough that a reader can propose a method against them. Section~\ref{sec:open}
is written as an invitation, not a commitment; every direction there carries an
explicit kill criterion so that a reader can judge whether it is worth pursuing.

\paragraph{Scope stated up front}
The empirical claims below are scoped to two released checkpoints
(\texttt{dino\_wm\_metaworld}, \texttt{jepa\_wm\_metaworld}), two contact tasks
(\texttt{mw-push}, \texttt{mw-pick-place}) with \texttt{mw-reach} as a
free-space control, one simulator, and the reported planner protocol. They are
not claims about joint-embedding architectures in general, about all latent
costs, or about what a differently trained encoder would do. Where the review in
Section~\ref{sec:lit} ranges wider than the evidence, it is labelled as review.

\section{Background and formal setup}\label{sec:background}
% ===============================================================

\paragraph{Joint-embedding prediction}
A joint-embedding predictive architecture trains a predictor to match the
representation of a target view rather than to reconstruct
it~\citep{lecun2022path,assran2023ijepa}. There is no decoder. This is a
deliberate design choice---it frees the model from spending capacity on
pixel-level detail---but it has a consequence that matters here: the latent is
not directly inspectable, so one cannot read off what a distance in that space
measures. Every statement in this document about ``what the cost is doing''
therefore required building a probe and validating it separately.

\paragraph{Action-conditioned prediction and planning}
Given a frozen encoder, an action-conditioned predictor is trained so that
$\hat{z}_{t+1} = f(z_t, a_t)$ matches the encoded next observation. Planning
minimises a terminal cost over an action horizon $H$,
\[
  a_{1:H}^\star \;=\; \arg\min_{a_{1:H}} \; c\!\left(f^{H}(z_t, a_{1:H}),\; z_g\right),
\]
where $z_g$ is the encoding of a goal image and $c$ is Euclidean distance for
every released baseline considered here.

\paragraph{The optimiser is not a passive sampler}
The cross-entropy method~\citep{rubinstein1997cem} and its practical descendants
\citep{pinneri2020icem} do not draw candidates i.i.d. They sample a population,
keep an elite fraction, \emph{refit} the sampling distribution to those elites,
and repeat. This refitting loop is load-bearing for everything that follows.
After several iterations the population is concentrated wherever the cost reads
low. If the region where the cost reads low is a region where the cost is
\emph{wrong}, the optimiser will move its own candidates into that region and
keep them there. This is why an average error measured on a fixed held-out set
does not bound the error the planner actually experiences, and why the phrase
``optimizer-conditioned'' is used throughout.

\paragraph{Three cost families}
Distinguishing these three is necessary to read any table below.
A \emph{proxy cost} is computable from the representation at deployment: latent
$L_2$, or a supervised readout of object and hand position from frozen tokens
(``stateprobe''). A \emph{reference cost} is computed from privileged simulator
state; it is a positive control for the search budget, never a deployable
method. \emph{Physical outcome} is the simulator's own task flag at the final
environment step.

\section{How to read this literature: three roles, three bars}\label{sec:roles}
% ===============================================================

A large fraction of the work closest to this problem consists of 2026 arXiv
preprints that have not been refereed. Treating them all at one level of trust
would be a mistake in either direction: dismissing them would ignore genuine
priority, and leaning on them would build arguments on unreviewed claims. The
review below therefore triages every paper by \emph{why it is being read}, and
holds each role to a different evidentiary bar.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.4em,style=nextline]
\item[Role (A): systems we run.] Credibility comes from the released checkpoint
and code that we reproduce ourselves, not from the venue. A model release may be
an unrefereed technical report and still be perfectly usable as an object of
study. The obligation is the reverse of citation: every number such a paper
reports must be independently verified before use.
\item[Role (B): foundations we stand on.] These carry arguments the programme
relies on, so they must be peer reviewed at a strong venue. They may be cited as
established. A conceptual source that is influential but unrefereed is marked
(B\,$\dagger$) and read as a set of assumptions rather than as evidence.
\item[Role (C): concurrent work we delimit against.] Venue is irrelevant. These
are read because a reviewer will ask what distinguishes this work from them, and
because they establish priority that no later claim may ignore. They are
\emph{never} cited as evidence for a claim of our own.
\end{description}

Of the twenty core papers in Table~\ref{tab:core}, twelve are peer reviewed at
ICML, ICLR, NeurIPS, CVPR, TMLR, CoRL, L4DC or \emph{Management Science}; eight
are preprints, and seven of those eight sit in role (C). The load-bearing
conceptual arguments are therefore carried almost entirely by refereed work,
which is the property this triage exists to make checkable.

\section{Literature review}\label{sec:lit}
% ===============================================================

The review is organised by \emph{what is being blamed for failure}, because that
is where a gap becomes visible to someone looking for a method. The four failure
surfaces are defined in Table~\ref{tab:surfaces}; the role tag from
Section~\ref{sec:roles} travels with each paper as an attribute rather than
determining which subsection it lands in. Within each subsection, paragraphs run
foundations (B), then systems (A), then concurrent work (C), and each subsection
closes with a paragraph stating what the cited work does and does not license.

\subsection{Foundations: joint-embedding prediction}\label{sec:lit-found}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\citet{lecun2022path} (B\,$\dagger$) is the origin of the programme's
architecture and of the assumption it tests. The document proposes prediction in
representation space and a planning module that minimises a learned cost through
a world model. Read it as a set of assumptions, not as evidence: it is a position
paper and was never refereed. The specific assumption under test in
Section~\ref{sec:evidence} is the implicit inference from ``good for
prediction'' to ``good for optimisation''.

\citet{assran2023ijepa} (B) makes the architecture concrete: predict the
representations of masked target blocks from a context block, with an
exponential-moving-average target encoder and no pixel reconstruction. The
mechanism worth carrying forward is the \emph{teacher-forced target}---the target
encoder sees the real future---and the collapse-avoidance machinery that
constrains what the latent geometry can be. \citet{bardes2022vicreg} (B) gives
the explicit variance--invariance--covariance form of that constraint. Neither
objective has a planner anywhere in the loop, which is the structural
observation this subsection exists to establish. \citet{lejepa2025} (C) continues
this formalisation on the training side.

On the systems side, \citet{assran2025vjepa2} (A) scales video JEPA and
post-trains an action-conditioned predictor for robot planning; it is the only
member of this family with real-robot results, and its planning cost is again
Euclidean distance to a goal embedding. Two cautions attach. First, the original
release shipped with an action-normalisation defect, and the corrected
checkpoint should be used. Second, \citet{murlabadia2026vjepa21} (C) reports that
a dense predictive loss and deeper self-supervision yield a twenty-point
improvement in real-robot grasping over V-JEPA 2-AC. That is a live alternative
explanation for contact failure---improve the features rather than the
cost---and Section~\ref{sec:threats} returns to it.
\citet{maes2026leworldmodel} (C) trains the encoder and predictor end to end from
pixels, which changes what ``frozen encoder'' even denotes;
\citet{causaljepa2026,invariantjepwm2026} (C) impose object-level and
planning-facing invariance structure at training time.

\paragraph{What this licenses}
Nothing about planning. It licenses exactly one statement: the latents used by
these planners are shaped by predictive and invariance objectives that never see
a planner, and no member of this family defines the cost the planner will later
query.

\subsection{Latent world models as planners: the systems under audit}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\citet{zhou2024dinowm} (A) is the canonical stack: freeze a DINOv2 encoder,
train only an action-conditioned predictor over patch tokens, and plan zero-shot
with CEM against the latent $L_2$ distance to a goal \emph{image}. Three details
matter for reading later tables---the latent is a patch-token field rather than a
single vector, the cost is summed over tokens, and the goal is specified
visually rather than as a state.

\citet{jepawms2025} (A) is the most load-bearing citation in this document. It
systematically ablates design choices for joint-embedding predictive world
models and, critically, releases the exact MetaWorld checkpoints and planning
stack audited in Section~\ref{sec:evidence}. Its planning configurations are
$L_2$ with CEM for every baseline, which is why $L_2$ is the cost under study
rather than an arbitrary choice. It answers what makes planning \emph{succeed};
the present programme answers the complementary question of which block fails.
Related latent-planning systems---\citet{sobal2025pldm} (C) on reward-free
offline data, \citet{nwm2024} (C) on navigation, and \citet{zhang2026hwm} (C) on
hierarchical decomposition---vary the data regime and the temporal structure
while keeping the same terminal-distance formulation.

\citet{hansen2024tdmpc2} (B) is the conceptual counterexample and should be read
as such. Its latent is trained jointly with reward and value, that is, \emph{for
control}, and its planner uses a learned terminal value inside MPPI. It does not
exhibit this failure in the same form, which is evidence that cost geometry is a
property one can train for rather than an inevitability.

\citet{yu2020metaworld} (B) supplies the evaluation substrate and must be read
for its definitions, not its breadth: success is the simulator's task flag,
evaluated here strictly at the final environment step rather than latched at any
step, with object-to-goal radii of $5$\,cm for push and $7$\,cm for pick-place.
\citet{rubinstein1997cem} and \citet{pinneri2020icem} (B) define the optimiser.

\paragraph{What this licenses}
That the audited stack is the field's standard one, and that reported successes
concentrate in free-space or saturating cells. It licenses no claim about
joint-embedding models in general. \emph{Gap:} aggregate closed-loop success is
essentially the only statistic these systems report. None reports
selection-level statistics on its own candidate population, which is precisely
the quantity Section~\ref{sec:evidence} measures.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Failure surface 1: prediction error}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\citet{janner2019trust} (B) established that a policy optimised inside a learned
model exploits that model's error, and that rollout length must be limited by
model confidence. \citet{lambert2020objective} (B) is the sharper statement for
this proposal: the objective used to train a dynamics model is not monotonically
related to control performance, so a model that is better by likelihood can
control worse. Everything in this document is a transposition of that argument
from the dynamics block to the cost block.

\citet{parthasarathy2025traintest} (B\,$\dagger$) identifies a second, distinct
channel: gradient-based planners query the model through input gradients, which
is not how it was trained, and therefore produce out-of-distribution or
adversarial action trajectories; they repair it with train-time synthesis.
\citet{bhamidipaty2026imperfect} (C) states the general form---imperfect world
models are exploitable---and is the closest published work by vocabulary, so it
must be cited explicitly rather than left implicit.

\paragraph{What this licenses}
That exploitation of \emph{dynamics} error is well established for both sampling
and gradient planners. \emph{Gap and consequence:} every result in this cluster
locates the defect in $f$. The audit design removes $f$ entirely by executing
candidates in the simulator, so this surface is closed by construction and
cannot explain the observed failure. That is the single most important reason
the audit is set up the way it is.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Failure surface 2: proposal and coverage}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\citet{imwm2026} (C) replaces the learned predictor with literal environment
rollout while holding the encoder, the terminal latent-MSE cost, the CEM budget
and the replanning cadence fixed. Two things follow, and the order matters.
First, \emph{oracle dynamics is theirs}: no later work, including this one, may
claim it as a novel construction. Second, their diagnosis differs from the one
reached here. Inspecting whether any goal-reaching candidate appeared in the CEM
population at all, they find that on their failing cells almost none did, which
makes the binding constraint proposal coverage rather than ranking; their
repairs are demonstration retrieval, a start--goal--action compatibility score,
and a reliability gate.

This is the principal competing explanation for the results in
Section~\ref{sec:evidence} and cannot be dismissed by narration; it has to be
measured on the same populations, which is why availability is reported in
Table~\ref{tab:mechanism}. \citet{nguyen2026latentgeometry} (C) attacks the same
surface from the opposite side by removing online search altogether in favour of
a goal-conditioned inverse-dynamics map, matching or beating CEM in seven of
eight settings at $100$--$130\times$ lower per-decision cost. Its sweep spans
CEM, MPPI, iCEM and gradient planners, and stands as the field's clearest
warning against drawing conclusions from a single optimiser.

\paragraph{What this licenses}
That coverage is a real and sometimes dominant failure mode, and that
attributing failure to ranking requires ruling it out. \emph{Gap:} coverage and
selection are almost never decomposed on the \emph{same} population. IMWM
measures availability, TRM measures ranking, and no work reports both jointly
against physical ground truth.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Failure surface 3: transition realizability and action grounding}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\citet{acid2026} (C) argues that terminal closeness does not ensure that
intermediate predicted transitions are realizable, and adds per-step
inverse-dynamics cycle residuals to the planning cost with an adaptive scale,
reporting gains across four action-conditioned world models and six tasks.
\citet{liu2026wav} (C) decomposes verification into state plausibility and action
reachability using a sparse inverse model and cycle consistency.
\citet{chen2026atm} (C) builds probes on real encoded versus model-predicted
transitions and produces a transfer matrix diagnosing whether transitions
preserve action-identifiable semantics, together with a screening score and a
training objective.

\citet{radha2026uwmjepa} (C) is relevant for one specific mechanism, and should
be read only for it: under a teacher-forced JEPA objective the target encoder has
already observed the trajectory containing the action's effect, which admits an
action-invariant solution in which the predictor matches the target without using
the action channel; counterfactual targets restore action sensitivity. Its
experimental setting is a small partially observed physics task, not robot
planning, so its evidentiary weight here is low even though its mechanism claim
is directly relevant. \citet{mirabench2026} and \citet{whatifworld2026} (C)
provide paired-intervention and action-following benchmarks for video world
models; \citet{xia2023neuralcausal} (B) is the peer-reviewed anchor for
counterfactual identification vocabulary.

\paragraph{What this licenses}
That action identifiability, counterfactual targets, inverse-consistency
verification, and paired physical interventions are all already published. No
priority claim survives in this cluster, which is the direct reason the present
programme retired its own action-grounding framing
(Section~\ref{sec:evidence-observational}). \emph{Gap:} every repair here is
measured under \emph{learned} dynamics, so an observed gain cannot be attributed
to the realizability surface rather than to ordinary dynamics-error reduction.
The discriminating experiment---the same repair under exact dynamics---is unrun
in the literature, and is direction D5.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Failure surface 4: cost geometry and selection under
optimisation}\label{sec:lit-cost}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

This is the surface the completed work lands on, and the longest subsection.

\paragraph{(a) Cost-geometry repairs in latent planning}
\citet{trm2026} (C) is the closest published work and must be represented
accurately, because it is easy to overstate the remaining novelty. Holding
encoder, dynamics, sampler, optimiser, budget and evaluation fixed, it replaces
or hybridises only the terminal cost, and reports: a raw latent cost that
misranks candidates despite nearly perfect linear decodability of task
coordinates; a true task-state cost reaching full success under identical
settings; a solver-stress control in which much more search fails to rescue the
wrong objective; same-candidate selection audits comparing cost orderings on
identical candidates; and a subspace intervention showing that the useful
coordinate rowspace accounts for under one percent of raw latent MSE. It also
deploys the repaired metric inside full CEM. Consequently, any statement that
prior work reports only aggregate success, or never audits candidate selection,
or never tests a repair under search, is false. \citet{rcaux2026} (C) makes the
adjacent argument that a candidate can be close in latent space yet unreachable
within a finite horizon, and adds a budget-conditioned reachability head.
\citet{straightening2026} (C) reshapes the representation directly so that
Euclidean latent distance better approximates geodesic distance, and
\citet{kim2026latentstate} (C) frames the general problem as latent state design
under sufficiency constraints---which of the two is the right frame is an open
question this proposal returns to in D3.

\paragraph{(b) Selection under noisy proxies}
This is the peer-reviewed backbone, and the most commonly skipped reading.
\citet{smith2006optimizer} (B) is the null hypothesis of the entire audit:
selecting the maximum over noisy estimates guarantees that the chosen item's
true value is worse than its estimate, \emph{even when every estimate is
unbiased}. It follows that a demonstration of proxy misranking means nothing
unless it exceeds a matched-noise baseline---which is why
Table~\ref{tab:mechanism} reports a residual-matched permutation null rather
than a raw regret. \citet{skalse2022defining} (B) formalises when improving a
proxy reduces true performance. \citet{gao2023overopt} (B) measures proxy against
gold reward as optimisation pressure increases and exhibits the characteristic
rise-then-fall; that curve shape is what licenses the word
\emph{overoptimisation}, and in its absence this document says \pocketsel{}
instead. \citet{karwowski2024goodhart} (B) supplies a measurable definition of
optimisation pressure, which is the abscissa of any budget sweep.

\paragraph{(c) Inference-time proxy exploitation elsewhere}
\citet{inferencetimehacking2025} and \citet{gamingverifiers2026} (C) report the
same shape for best-of-$n$ and verifier-guided selection in language models.
The analogy is therefore established and ``Goodhart under search'' is not itself
a novel observation.

\paragraph{What this licenses}
The vocabulary---proxy, reference, optimisation pressure---and a methodological
requirement: no misranking claim without a matched null. \emph{Gap:} the Goodhart
literature never has physical ground truth for the selected candidate, and the
latent-planning literature never has an optimiser-independent truth channel. The
intersection, exact candidate dynamics together with simulator-state truth on the
optimiser's own realised population, is the seam the completed work occupies.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Evaluation protocol: what counts as evidence}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\citet{yu2026wmeval} (C) argues that predictive quality should be connected to
downstream decision quality rather than reported in isolation.
\citet{hiddenfailure2026} (C) identifies a protocol confound this programme must
rule out: terminal-at-horizon scoring can be misaligned with prefix execution
under receding-horizon control, so planning horizon, executed prefix,
terminal versus running score, and replanning cadence must all be reported.
\citet{wmsurvey2026} (C) provides the wider taxonomy for orientation.

\paragraph{What this licenses}
That protocol details are first-class. \emph{Gap:} no shared protocol reports
horizon, executed prefix, coverage, selection regret and rank correlation
together, which makes results across these papers formally incomparable. That
absence is itself proposable, as D6.

% ===============================================================
\section{Open research directions}\label{sec:open}
% ===============================================================

Six directions. Each carries a mandatory \textbf{What already constrains it}
field: if nothing constrains a direction, it is a wish rather than a direction,
and it does not belong here. Table~\ref{tab:directions} is the compressed index.

\subsection*{D1 --- Group-relative selection objectives}

\dfield{Hypothesis.} A cost trained to preserve the \emph{ordering} of
candidates within the optimiser's own populations, rather than to regress their
values, crosses contact success where value-fidelity objectives do not.

\dfield{What opens it.} The cost is the only block whose replacement flips the
outcome (Table~\ref{tab:terminal}: $64/64$ versus $0/64$ at identical budget).

\dfield{What already constrains it.} The selection-aware encoder sprint already
ran four arms at pilot scale and returned \texttt{STOP\_METHOD\_DIRECTION}. The
scalar softmin selection-regret objective gave push $[0,0,0]/16$; direct
regression to true cost gave $[1,0,2]/16$. Reach was preserved at 14--16/16
across arms, so the failure is not lost capacity. The one thread that survives
is narrow but real: the \emph{pairwise ranking} arm produced both the best
validation selection regret ($0.0020$ versus $0.0069$ for regression) and the
only non-zero seed ($5/16$).

\dfield{Falsifiable prediction.} A pairwise or listwise objective trained on at
least $5\times$ the mined populations reaches $\ge 8/16$ push in $\ge 3/3$
training seeds while preserving reach $\ge 13/16$.

\dfield{Minimal decisive experiment.} Four arms---pairwise, listwise softmin,
regression control, frozen control---on \texttt{dino\_wm\_metaworld} push, with
trainable capacity held identical across arms, disjoint mining and evaluation
episodes, and the primary readout reported per training seed.

\dfield{Kill criterion.} Pairwise indistinguishable from regression at $5\times$
data, or an apparent gain carried by a single seed.

\subsection*{D2 --- The predictor axis, evaluated at the planning level}

\dfield{Hypothesis.} A counterfactual-target predictor objective, which repairs
action sensitivity offline, changes closed-loop contact success under
\emph{learned} dynamics.

\dfield{What opens it.} The offline gains on DINO-WM were substantial (CRA
$0.036 \rightarrow 0.233$; action error $1.492 \rightarrow 1.085$), and the
predictor axis was bypassed by construction in the oracle ladder, so it is
untested at the planning level.

\dfield{What already constrains it.} The same objective was mixed on JEPA-WM
(action error $1.337 \rightarrow 1.344$), which blocks a general method claim.
The objective family is also already published~\citep{radha2026uwmjepa,
chen2026atm}, so any contribution here is the closed-loop evaluation, not the
objective.

\dfield{Falsifiable prediction.} Closed-loop contact success changes by
$\ge 4/16$ on at least one checkpoint.

\dfield{Kill criterion.} Offline metrics improve while planning stays inside the
$0$--$2/16$ band on both checkpoints. Note that this outcome is publishable in
its own right: it would be the tenth entry in Table~\ref{tab:nulls} and would
close the predictor axis as firmly as the cost axis is now closed.

\subsection*{D3 --- Decision-equivalent representation objectives}

\dfield{Hypothesis.} The missing constraint is not fidelity but decision
equivalence: a representation should be trained so that the induced cost
preserves the \emph{argmin} over a goal family, discarding whatever the decision
does not use.

\dfield{What opens it.} The signature across Table~\ref{tab:nulls}---nine
fidelity improvements, zero planning improvements. The literature anchor is
\citet{kim2026latentstate}, which frames world-model design as latent state
choice under sufficiency constraints, and \citet{straightening2026}, which
reshapes geometry so Euclidean latent distance approximates the quantity
planning actually needs.

\dfield{What already constrains it.} D1's sprint is a weak instance of this idea
applied at the encoder tail, and it stopped. Any stronger version must explain
why it is not simply that experiment with more parameters. The concept is also
close to what TD-MPC2~\citep{hansen2024tdmpc2} already achieves by training the
latent jointly with value, so the novelty must be argued in the goal-conditioned,
reward-free setting rather than assumed.

\dfield{Falsifiable prediction.} An explicit decision-equivalence objective
preserves the reference-optimal choice on $\ge 95\%$ of held-out decision points
where a value-fidelity objective at matched capacity preserves it on
substantially fewer, and this transfers to $\ge 8/16$ contact success.

\dfield{Kill criterion.} Decision-point preservation improves while contact
success stays in the frozen band---which would make decision equivalence the
tenth fidelity metric to dissociate from planning.

\subsection*{D4 --- Hierarchy and subgoals rather than flat amortisation}

\dfield{Hypothesis.} A two-level decomposition with learned subgoals crosses
contact success where both flat CEM and flat amortised control fail, because
each level faces a shorter-horizon cost with less room to be exploited.

\dfield{What opens it.} \citet{nguyen2026latentgeometry} matched CEM in seven of
eight settings at $100$--$130\times$ lower cost, and \citet{zhang2026hwm}
demonstrates hierarchical latent planning in this exact model family.

\dfield{What already constrains it.} The programme's own amortised control
experiment is a null---the object never moves---so removing search alone does
not cross the wall. A hierarchy that inherits the same terminal-cost geometry at
every level has no obvious reason to behave differently.

\dfield{Falsifiable prediction.} $\ge 8/16$ push where flat CEM and flat GC-IDM
both sit at $0$--$2/16$.

\dfield{Kill criterion.} Subgoal decomposition stays inside the frozen band,
which would localise the defect to the cost at every level of the hierarchy---a
strong result either way.

\subsection*{D5 --- The missing discriminating experiment: repairs under exact dynamics}

\dfield{Hypothesis.} Repairs that target transition realizability lose most of
their reported gain when candidate dynamics are made exact, because they are in
fact repairing dynamics error.

\dfield{What opens it.} The \emph{Dynamics} column of Table~\ref{tab:matrix}:
ACID, WAV, ATM and RC-aux all report gains under learned dynamics only. The
experiment that separates the two attributions has not been run by anyone.

\dfield{What already constrains it.} The programme's own attempt at a
concurrent-method adaptation was excluded from the submission precisely because
its null lacked adaptation-specific positive controls. A null here is
uninterpretable without first demonstrating that the reimplementation reproduces
the original paper's gain under \emph{learned} dynamics. That positive control is
a prerequisite, not an optional extra.

\dfield{Falsifiable prediction.} At least one realizability repair loses
$\ge 80\%$ of its reported gain under exact dynamics.

\dfield{Kill criterion.} A repair survives exact dynamics. This is the single
most valuable counterexample to the programme's thesis and must be reported as
prominently as a confirmation.

\subsection*{D6 --- An optimizer-conditioned evaluation protocol}

\dfield{Hypothesis.} The selection-regret-beyond-null signature is a general
property of representation-derived costs under selection pressure, not a CEM
pathology, and can be standardised into a protocol that makes results across
this literature comparable.

\dfield{What opens it.} Seams S1--S3: no shared protocol reports horizon,
executed prefix, coverage, selection regret and rank correlation together, so
results across these papers are formally incomparable.

\dfield{What already constrains it.} The programme's own planner control is an
\emph{endpoint} control (CEM 1/16, MPPI 0/16, random shooting 2/16) that
compares outcomes, not mechanisms. Mechanism generality across optimisers is
genuinely unestablished, and single-checkpoint conclusions are exactly what this
literature warns against.

\dfield{Falsifiable prediction.} The signature replicates in $\ge 2$ non-CEM
optimisers and $\ge 2$ checkpoint families, with the coverage/selection
decomposition computed on identical populations.

\dfield{Kill criterion.} The signature is CEM-specific, which reframes the
finding as a CEM pathology and must be stated as such.

\paragraph{Anti-directions}
Three things the evidence says not to attempt again in this harness. First,
post-hoc cost repair on the frozen encoder: six variants, all inside
$0$--$2/16$. Second, naive ensemble-disagreement pessimism: the standard
model-based remedy is flat here because ensemble members share the frozen base's
blind spot, so disagreement stays low exactly where the cost is wrong. Third,
improving decoding accuracy as a proxy target: the off-policy-robust probe took
object decoding from $78\%$ to $91.5\%$ below $5$\,cm and transferred zero
planning success.

% ===============================================================
\section{Threats to validity and claim discipline}\label{sec:threats}
% ===============================================================

\paragraph{The strongest alternative explanation}
\citet{murlabadia2026vjepa21} reports that dense predictive features improve
real-robot grasping by twenty points over the previous generation of the same
model family. If contact failure is caused by insufficient spatial density in
the frozen features rather than by cost geometry, then the correct intervention
is a better encoder, and the audit would be measuring a property of two
older released checkpoints rather than of latent planning. This programme cannot
currently distinguish the two, which is why every scale statement is written as
\scopedscale{}. Testing a dense-feature checkpoint through the same audit is the
highest-value single extension available.

\paragraph{Other limits}
Two checkpoints from one design lineage; one simulator; one task family;
uncertainty clustered over environment seeds, which is not uncertainty over
checkpoint training seeds; a privileged reference cost that is a control and not
a method; and a literature base in which the closest works are unrefereed 2026
preprints whose reported numbers have not been independently reproduced here.
