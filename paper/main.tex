\begin{abstract}
Action-conditioned JEPA world models couple predicted latent futures to a
representation-induced goal cost and a test-time optimizer. We isolate a
failure at this optimizer--cost interface on contact-rich MetaWorld tasks. An
\emph{oracle ladder} rolls every candidate through the simulator and re-encodes
the resulting observation, removing learned-dynamics error while varying only
the cost. With the same planning budget, privileged true-state cost solves
push $63/64$ and pick-place $41/64$; latent $L_2$ solves $0/64$ in all four
checkpoint--task cells, and a probe implementation of the state cost reaches at
most $4/64$. On identical first-iteration populations, probe selection incurs
$2.05$--$2.47$\,cm simulator-state shaped-cost opportunity regret. A matched-residual permutation
null preserves the same error magnitudes but incurs only $1.07$--$1.15$\,cm;
actual-minus-null regret is positive in all four cells. A paired branch
intervention starts from identical candidates and changes only the refitting
cost; after five refits, the probe branch's best available simulator-state cost is
$1.51$--$2.01$\,cm worse across both checkpoints and tasks. Exact-success
coverage is also sparse. Finally, a held-out TRM-style terminal-ranking head
fails to cross contact in either replacement or hybrid form ($0/64$ across all
tested cells), while its privileged control remains strong. We contribute a
simulator-verified audit that separates dynamics, proposal coverage, immediate
ranking, and adaptive refitting. The claim is scoped to the tested tasks,
checkpoints, costs, and planners; it neither attributes the defect uniquely to
the encoder nor rules out better learned costs or proposal mechanisms.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Action-conditioned world models in the JEPA family
(DINO-WM~\citep{zhou2024dinowm}, V-JEPA-2-AC~\citep{assran2025vjepa2}, and the
design study of~\citet{jepawms2025}) combine a latent predictor, a goal cost,
and test-time sampling. Their strongest closed-loop results are on reaching and
pose control; contact-rich manipulation remains brittle. One common explanation
is learned-dynamics error. We ask a narrower question: \emph{if candidate
futures are exact, can the representation-derived cost still cause search to
fail?}

This distinction matters because a planner consumes an \emph{ordering} over
its own candidate population, not average prediction or decoding error. A cost
can be useful off policy yet assign its smallest values to physically inferior
candidates under optimization. This is compatible with optimizer's curse and
Goodhart effects~\citep{gao2023overopt,skalse2022defining,
karwowski2024goodhart}, but the existence of those effects in principle does
not locate them in a robot world-model stack.

We isolate the interface in MetaWorld. Every candidate action sequence is
executed from a simulator snapshot and its endpoint is re-encoded, so the
planner sees exact environment dynamics through the frozen representation. We
then vary the terminal cost, score identical populations by both proxy and
simulator truth, and fork adaptive CEM from a shared initial population. This
design separates four hypotheses:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{dynamics}: does failure persist after removing the learned
  predictor?
  \item \emph{budget}: can the same finite planner solve the task with an
  informative privileged cost?
  \item \emph{coverage}: does the population contain physically successful
  candidates?
  \item \emph{ranking}: when better physical candidates are present, does the
  learned cost select them and preserve them through refitting?
\end{enumerate}

The resulting evidence has three parts. First, a locked 64-seed oracle ladder
shows that perfect dynamics plus true-state cost solves push $63/64$ and
pick-place $41/64$, while every tested frozen-representation cost remains near
failure on two released checkpoints. Second, on identical candidates the probe
cost incurs CI-clean simulator-state shaped-cost opportunity regret beyond a matched-noise null;
changing only the refitting cost changes the best simulator-state candidate available
after five iterations. Third, exact-success coverage is sparse, and a held-out
TRM-style terminal-ranking adaptation does not cross the contact wall. The
supported mechanism is therefore joint coverage and cost-ranking failure, not
a unique encoder defect or a universal indictment of latent planning.

Planning success is reported as solved episodes out of the stated paired seed
set. Workspace distances are in metres unless converted to centimetres. A CEM
budget ``$100\times6$'' means 100 candidates over six refitting iterations per
replan, with rollout horizon stated separately.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{JEPA world models for planning.}
DINO-WM~\citep{zhou2024dinowm} predicts forward in the frozen feature space of a
self-supervised image encoder and plans with sampling-based MPC from a goal
image; V-JEPA-2-AC~\citep{assran2025vjepa2} learns an action-conditioned world
model on a video encoder and demonstrates zero-shot manipulation;
\citet{jepawms2025} systematically study the design choices that make such
models plan. We take these as frozen baselines and treat their own evaluations
as evidence: across all three, strong closed-loop results are pose/free-space
tasks, and contact is where the story thins (hand-crafted sub-goals, qualitative
figures, ``hallucinated grasping''). V-JEPA~2.1~\citep{vjepa21_2026} subsequently
improves dense spatial features and reports a large real-robot grasping gain,
making representation quality and observability an important alternative
explanation. Our study does not rule that out; it asks whether the costs induced
by the released planning checkpoints survive their optimizer's own selection.

\paragraph{Overoptimization and reward hacking.}
Optimizing against a learned proxy eventually decouples proxy from truth:
formalized for RLHF reward models by~\citet{gao2023overopt}, characterized
in general by~\citet{skalse2022defining} and~\citet{karwowski2024goodhart},
and anticipated as ``reward hacking''~\citep{amodei2016concrete}. We use the
same mathematical lens without claiming the general phenomenon as new: CEM
optimizes a proxy terminal cost, and finite-population selection can concentrate
favorable residuals. Our contribution is the controlled measurement available
in simulation: dynamics can be held perfect, every candidate can be scored by
physical truth, and the cost used for adaptive refitting can be intervened on.

\paragraph{Model exploitation in model-based RL.}
That policy optimization exploits errors in learned \emph{dynamics} is a classic
MBRL concern~\citep{janner2019trust}, running through the sampling-MPC lineage
our CEM planner inherits~\citep{chua2018pets, nagabandi2019pddm}, related to
the objective mismatch between likelihood training and control
performance~\citep{lambert2020objective}, and addressed offline with
pessimism~\citep{yu2020mopo, kidambi2020morel}; learned agents more broadly
admit adversarial exploitation of their blind spots~\citep{gleave2020adversarial}.
Concurrently, \citet{parthasarathy2025traintest} show \emph{gradient-based}
planners exploit a world model's input gradients and respond by smoothing the
dynamics. Our finding is materially different: with dynamics oracle-controlled,
the representation-derived terminal cost can still misrank candidates.

\paragraph{Closest cost and planner-interface diagnoses.} Several concurrent
arXiv preprints built on LeWorldModel~\citep{leworldmodel2026} reach compatible
diagnoses by different routes; we treat them as important concurrent work, not
accepted venue results. RC-aux~\citep{rcaux2026} makes the framing
explicit --- ``predictive but not plannable'': accurate short-horizon
prediction can still leave a latent whose Euclidean distance does not reflect
finite-horizon reachability --- and repairs it with a training-time
reachability objective that reshapes the representation. Trajectory
reachability metrics (TRM)~\citep{trm2026}
instead add a post-hoc terminal-ranking head on the frozen backbone. TRM is the
closest cost-side study: it deploys the repaired metric inside CEM, audits
ordering on identical candidates, compares against true-state cost, intervenes
on the task-relevant subspace, and stress-tests larger search budgets.
ACID~\citep{acid2026} argues, in the same spirit,
that a terminal-only latent cost cannot see whether intermediate transitions
are realizable, and adds an inverse-dynamics cycle-consistency term to the
planning cost. IMWM~\citep{imwm2026} replaces learned dynamics with literal
environment rollout and finds many failures arise because no successful
candidate enters the finite CEM population. Thus neither latent-cost
misranking nor oracle-dynamics failure alone is our novelty.

Our narrower target is a contact-rich robot regime in which we jointly vary
dynamics and cost, inspect optimizer-selected elites in simulator object state,
and measure candidate coverage and cost-based selection separately. Relative to
IMWM, contact populations in our harness are often coverage-limited as well;
when physical alternatives are present, opportunity regret additionally exposes
misranking. Relative to TRM, our question is whether a learned terminal ranking
survives fresh adaptive search on held-out MetaWorld episodes. Our TRM-style
adaptation provides a direct, scoped comparison (Table~\ref{tab:trm}).

\paragraph{Reconciling coverage, ranking, and search.}
The concurrent accounts need not be mutually exclusive. IMWM identifies a
finite-query coverage bottleneck and adds demonstration priors;
TRM repairs terminal ranking. Our experiments measure coverage and ranking in
one contact harness. Exact successful candidates are sparse, so coverage matters. Yet on
identical populations the proxy-selected candidate also has positive
simulator-state shaped-cost opportunity regret, and branch-specific refitting
worsens the best simulator-state candidate subsequently available. The
supported conclusion is therefore a
joint coverage-and-ranking bottleneck, not a universal claim that search itself
is either necessary or harmful.

\paragraph{Benchmarks.}
We evaluate on Metaworld~\citep{yu2020metaworld}, whose simulator state lets us
verify every candidate cost against physical ground truth. Planning follows the
upstream CEM configuration~\citep{rubinstein1997cem}.

\section{Cost remains limiting under oracle dynamics}\label{sec:ladder}

We isolate which component breaks contact planning by swapping exactly one rung
at a time, holding the planner fixed (CEM $100\times 6$ per replan, $H{=}6$,
strict episode-end success). Headline arms use 64 unseen paired seeds on both
\texttt{dino\_wm\_metaworld} and \texttt{jepa\_wm\_metaworld}. This budget is
smaller than the parity harness of Sec.~\ref{sec:planning}
($300\times15$) for compute reasons; the privileged row is the within-protocol
test that the finite budget admits success.
The key device is the \emph{latent oracle}: every CEM candidate is rolled in the
\emph{simulator}, rendered, and encoded by the real frozen encoder --- perfect
dynamics through the true representation, learned predictor removed. Any rung
that fails under the latent oracle cannot attribute that failure to learned
dynamics. It does not, by itself, distinguish cost misranking from insufficient
proposal coverage; Sec.~\ref{sec:adversary} measures both.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\caption{Locked oracle ladder (64 unseen paired seeds; strict episode-end
success). Every row uses the same CEM budget and simulator dynamics. The
privileged cost demonstrates that the task and budget admit success; replacing
simulator state by frozen-representation costs collapses both checkpoints.}
\label{tab:ladder}
\begin{tabular}{lllcc}
\toprule
rung & model & cost & push & pick \\
\midrule
state oracle & --- & true state & \textbf{63/64} & \textbf{41/64} \\
latent oracle & DINO-WM & latent $L_2$ & 0/64 & 0/64 \\
latent oracle & DINO-WM & probe state cost & 4/64 & 0/64 \\
latent oracle & JEPA-WM & latent $L_2$ & 0/64 & 0/64 \\
latent oracle & JEPA-WM & probe state cost & 1/64 & 0/64 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The ladder establishes two points. First, learned-dynamics error is not
\emph{necessary} for failure in this harness: every candidate endpoint is a
true simulator future, yet the representation-derived costs remain near zero.
Second, the planner implementation and finite budget cannot by themselves
explain the contrast between scorers: with privileged state cost, the same
search succeeds on $98.4\%$ of push and $64.1\%$ of pick episodes. The representation-derived cost remains
limiting, but later coverage measurements show that it is not the only limit.
Nor does this result prove that the encoder alone lacks information: the tested
interface contains the encoder, readout, training distribution, composite cost,
and planner-induced proposal distribution.

\paragraph{Object-only decoding is not a certificate for the state cost.}
On random-action off-policy frames, a hardened object probe reaches median
$2.01$\,cm error and $91.5\%$ within $5$\,cm. We do not call the whole cost
accurate on this basis. The end-effector probe has median $5.14$\,cm error and
only $45.8\%$ within $5$\,cm; the decoded hand--object relative vector has
median $11.18$\,cm error and only $3.7\%$ within $5$\,cm. Since the probe cost is
$\|\hat o-\hat g\|+0.5\|\hat h-\hat o\|$, this relative geometry is directly
task-relevant. Improving the object-only metric from $78.3\%$ to $91.5\%$
within $5$\,cm still yields only $1/16$ push in the original re-gate. The
supported conclusion is that coarse object localization alone is insufficient,
not that an otherwise precise cost mysteriously fails.

\section{Optimizer-conditioned cost misranking}\label{sec:adversary}

\subsection{Same-population selection before adaptive refitting}
\label{sec:exploit}
The oracle success gap alone says only that the probe cost is inaccurate. To
test whether selection concentrates favorable errors, we inspect the first CEM
population \emph{before} any refit. Every one of the same 100 action sequences
is rolled in MuJoCo and assigned both the stateprobe proxy $\hat c$ and the
simulator-state cost $c^*$. We then compare the top $10\%$ under $\hat c$, the
top $10\%$ under $c^*$, and the full population. Define opportunity regret
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{sel}} = c^*\!\left(\arg\min_a \hat c(a)\right)
                  - \min_a c^*(a),
\end{equation}
and optimism $o(a)=c^*(a)-\hat c(a)$. Both costs use the same object-plus-hand
functional form and metre units; their difference is induced by decoded versus
simulator state. Thus $R_{\mathrm{sel}}$ is simulator-state shaped-cost
opportunity regret within the sampled population, not return or closed-loop
policy regret.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\caption{First CEM population, 16 unseen seeds per cell. Object decode error is
the mean in cm. Optimism enrichment compares proxy elites with the full
population. All reported enrichments and regrets have seed-clustered 95\% CIs
strictly above zero.}
\label{tab:presel}
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
cell & population & proxy elite & truth elite & optimism enrich. & $R_{\rm sel}$ \\
\midrule
DINO push & 5.18 & 6.45 & 5.50 & 2.33\,cm & 2.47\,cm \\
DINO pick & 4.98 & 6.23 & 5.17 & 2.16\,cm & 2.25\,cm \\
JEPA push & 4.79 & 6.03 & 5.12 & 2.28\,cm & 2.05\,cm \\
JEPA pick & 4.78 & 6.18 & 5.16 & 2.21\,cm & 2.06\,cm \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Proxy elites have $1.24$--$1.40$\,cm more object error than their own raw
population and $0.91$--$1.06$\,cm more than simulator-truth elites on the same
candidates. More importantly, their optimism is enriched by
$2.16$--$2.33$\,cm and the selected action incurs $2.05$--$2.47$\,cm
simulator-state shaped-cost opportunity regret. Thus the low proxy ranking is
partly produced by residuals
with the favorable sign, rather than by physical progress alone. This is an
optimizer-conditioned selection effect in the representation--readout--cost
composition; it does not assign the error specifically to the encoder.

To distinguish structured misranking from the winner's curse that arises when
minimizing any noisy score, we construct a matched-residual permutation null.
Within each identical first-iteration population, we keep the simulator cost
and the exact multiset of stateprobe residuals, but randomly permute residuals
across candidates before selecting the minimum. The shuffled score therefore
has the same marginal error distribution and search budget while breaking the
association between residual sign and candidate geometry. Its argmin regret is
$1.07$--$1.15$\,cm, whereas the real score incurs $2.05$--$2.48$\,cm; the
actual-minus-null excess is $0.91$--$1.36$\,cm, with seed-clustered 95\% CIs
excluding zero in all four cells. Thus ordinary selection over a noisy cost
explains part, but not all, of the observed regret.

\subsection{Shared-population branch intervention}
Same-population regret establishes immediate misselection but not the
consequence of adaptive refitting. We therefore fork CEM at the first
population: both branches start from the identical simulator snapshot and
candidate-for-candidate identical actions; one refits on stateprobe cost and
the other on simulator-state cost. Later iterations use common Gaussian noise
transformed by each branch's own mean and variance. All candidates continue to
be rolled in MuJoCo and scored by both objectives.

Thus the cost used for selection changes what the optimizer proposes later:
after five refits, the stateprobe branch contains a worse \emph{best}
simulator-state candidate in every tested cell. The branch difference in population decode
error is CI-clean only for DINO push/pick, so we do not claim a universal
monotonic decoder-error drift. The robust consequence is degradation in best
simulator-state cost.

\subsection{Coverage and ranking are simultaneous bottlenecks}
An IMWM-style coverage audit asks whether an exactly successful candidate is
present before blaming selection. At the final CEM iteration, exact-success
coverage is only $8.0\%$ of replans for DINO push, $3.6\%$ for DINO pick, and
$0\%$ for both JEPA contact cells. Consequently, many failures cannot be
repaired by reranking the existing population. Yet selected simulator-state
shaped-cost regret is positive and rank agreement remains low on the same
candidates. Because final populations already reflect earlier cost-guided
refits, this is a joint coverage-and-ranking decomposition, not a pure
proposal-only intervention. We therefore retain a joint conclusion: proposal
coverage limits contact planning, and the representation-derived cost
additionally misranks available simulator-state progress. Neither is claimed as
the sole bottleneck.

\subsection{Planner robustness check}
On DINO-WM push under oracle dynamics, the probe cost remains near failure with
CEM ($1/16$), MPPI ($0/16$), and random shooting ($2/16$). Their free-space
Reach controls are $16/16$, $13/16$, and $6/16$, respectively. This rules out a
CEM-specific success artifact, but it is only an end-to-end robustness check:
the planners have different effective control strength, and the
same-population mechanism audit above is replicated only for CEM.

\subsection{A held-out terminal-ranking repair does not cross contact}
Trajectory reachability metrics (TRM) are the closest cost-side alternative:
they train a terminal-ranking head from temporal order and deploy it in CEM.
We evaluate a TRM-style adaptation on fresh simulator seeds $30000$--$30063$
with an immutable trajectory split and validation-only checkpoint selection.
Because large JEPA token grids are mean/max pooled before a symmetric pair MLP,
this is not an exact reproduction of the original architecture.

\section{Discussion, scope, and limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Optimizer-conditioned audits.}
The primary audit uses one candidate population and two scorers: proxy
$\hat c$ and simulator truth $c^*$. It reports (i)~rank correlation and top-$k$
overlap, (ii)~opportunity regret $R_{\rm sel}$, (iii)~optimism enrichment
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{\rm opt}=
 \mathbb{E}[c^*-\hat c\mid \text{proxy elite}]
 -\mathbb{E}[c^*-\hat c\mid \text{population}],
\end{equation}
and (iv)~whether an exactly successful candidate was covered before selection.
These answer distinct questions. Positive regret identifies misranking among
available candidates; low coverage identifies a proposal limitation; neither
alone predicts closed-loop success.

The shared-branch intervention adds the adaptive test: hold the initial
population and common noise fixed, vary the cost used for refitting, and compare
the best simulator-state cost subsequently available. This turns the audit from
a post-hoc correlation into an intervention on the planner--cost interface.
The result remains a mechanism diagnostic rather than a universal calibrated
metric; task success is the endpoint.

\paragraph{What follows for method design.}
The evidence motivates three non-exclusive responses: learn a cost whose
\emph{candidate ordering} is validated under adaptive search; improve proposal
coverage with demonstrations or action priors; and train task-relevant relative
state rather than optimizing an object-only decoding metric. The tested
TRM-style cost does not cross contact in this adaptation, but this is one
architecture and protocol rather than an impossibility result for learned
reachability costs.

\paragraph{Scope and honest limits.}
The cost-misranking mechanism is established on MetaWorld, where simulator state
lets us verify candidate ordering against ground truth. It has not been
replicated on a physical robot, and the same-population mechanism audit has not
been repeated for MPPI or shooting. The two tasks and two checkpoints do not
support a universal claim across environments, architectures, or scale. The
Reach parity check compares one released checkpoint over 16 episodes with a
published 3-seed$\times$96-episode result; it is a compatibility check, not a
seed-matched replication. The privileged cost is informative but unavailable at
deployment. The learned costs do not distinguish information absent from the
encoder from information inaccessible to the readout or corrupted by its
training distribution. Finally, exact-success coverage is only $0$--$8\%$ in
the audited final populations, which already depend on earlier cost-guided
refits. Cost misranking is therefore an additional measured bottleneck, not a
complete explanation of contact failure, and a robust vision-only cost remains
an open method question.

\section{Conclusion}
On the tested MetaWorld contact tasks, planning failure persists when learned
dynamics are replaced by simulator-perfect rollouts, while the same finite
planner succeeds with privileged state cost. On identical populations, the
probe cost selects candidates with optimistic residuals and positive
simulator-state shaped-cost regret; changing only the refitting cost
subsequently changes the best simulator-state candidate available. Exact
success coverage is also sparse, so the diagnosis is a joint
proposal-and-ranking bottleneck. The result is not a universal
impossibility claim about encoders, latent costs, scale, or grounding. It is a
controlled argument that world-model planners should report simulator-state
candidate ordering, shaped-cost opportunity regret, and coverage under the
optimizer's own proposal distribution, not object-only decoding or aggregate
prediction metrics alone.

\section{Audit and intervention details}\label{app:audit}
For each candidate action sequence $a$, the latent-oracle harness restores the
same MuJoCo snapshot, executes $a$, renders the endpoint, and encodes that image
with the released frozen encoder. The deployed proxy $\hat c(a)$ uses decoded
object and end-effector state; $c^*(a)$ applies the same object-plus-hand cost
form to simulator state. Simulator state is used only for evaluation or for the
explicit privileged branch.

The first-population audit evaluates 100 candidate sequences per seed before
any CEM refit. Opportunity regret is
$c^*(\arg\min_a\hat c(a))-\min_a c^*(a)$. The matched-residual null writes
$\hat c(a)=c^*(a)+r(a)$, permutes the observed residual multiset within each
population, and repeats selection. It therefore preserves the population,
search budget, true costs, and marginal error magnitudes while removing their
candidate-specific association. Confidence intervals cluster at the simulator
seed level.

For the adaptive intervention, both CEM branches share the initial snapshot,
first candidate population, and base Gaussian noise. One branch refits on
$\hat c$ and the other on $c^*$. The common noise is transformed by each
branch's own fitted mean and variance, so later populations differ only through
the intervened refitting cost. Every later candidate remains simulator-scored.
Coverage is measured separately as the fraction of replans whose final
population contains at least one candidate satisfying the simulator's exact
task-success predicate.

\section{Model, cost, and TRM details}\label{app:models}
DINO-WM and JEPA-WM are loaded through the released \texttt{EncPredWM} API and
their own preprocessing and action normalization. The latent-$L_2$ arm uses the
shipped goal distance. The stateprobe arm decodes object and end-effector
position from frozen endpoint tokens and applies the same state-cost form as the
privileged control. On random-action off-policy frames, object median error is
$2.01$\,cm, but end-effector median error is $5.14$\,cm and hand--object relative
error is $11.18$\,cm; object decoding alone is therefore not a certificate for
the composite planning cost.

The TRM-style heads use an immutable trajectory-level split, validation-only
checkpoint selection, and test-only planning episodes. Large token grids are
mean/max pooled before the symmetric pair MLP. Three independently trained
heads are evaluated separately; because they share the same 64 episode seeds,
their outcomes are paired replications and are not pooled into 192 independent
trials.

\section{Reproduction integrity}\label{app:repro}
Three model-agnostic environment mismatches were corrected before evaluation:
the offscreen renderer had to be reconstructed after camera assignment; the
released training frames required the upstream vertical flip; and the goal had
to use the scripted expert's final frame rather than its first success frame.
The Reach parity result in Table~\ref{tab:closedloop} is the committed
end-to-end control. Detailed calibration observations remain report-backed
rather than separately persisted artifacts and are not used as paper evidence
here.
