Attributing Failure to Representation, Search, or Cost}
\author{Nhat Nguyen Cuong}
\date{Research Proposal \quad$\cdot$\quad August 2026}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent\small
Robots that plan directly from camera images have converged on one architecture:
an encoder maps images to a latent state, an action-conditioned predictor rolls
that state forward, and an optimiser searches action sequences for the one whose
predicted outcome minimises a distance to a goal image. Its four blocks are
studied in separate literatures but evaluated by one joint statistic ---
end-to-end success rate --- so failures cannot be traced to a cause. This
proposal builds an instrument that makes the attribution tractable: a taxonomy
of four failure surfaces, each carrying an explicit condition that would refute
attributing a failure to it. Read against the literature, it exposes a
methodological gap --- nearly every published repair is evaluated under learned
dynamics, so its gain cannot be separated from ordinary prediction error. A
completed pilot runs the discriminating experiment for one surface and finds
that removing prediction error does not close the gap, motivating a programme on
costs and representations trained on the distribution the planner induces.
\end{abstract}

% ================================================================
\section{Introduction}
% ================================================================

Modern visual world models descend from the observation that a compact latent
state suffices to train a controller inside a model's own
imagination~\citep{ha2018worldmodels} and can be planned in directly from
pixels~\citep{hafner2019planet}. The field has since split along two axes that
are rarely co-designed. One shapes the latent: reconstruction-based latent
dynamics~\citep{hafner2025dreamerv3}, joint-embedding predictive
architectures~\citep{lecun2022path,assran2025vjepa2}, frozen pretrained visual
features~\citep{zhou2024dinowm}, and value-aware
latents~\citep{hansen2024tdmpc2}. The other searches over it: sampling-based
MPC~\citep{rubinstein1997cem,williams2017mppi}, gradient-based
planning, amortised control, and hierarchical
decomposition~\citep{zhang2026hwm}.

Failures are reported against the composition of the two, which is why the
diagnostic literature disagrees about mechanism: prediction error and its
exploitation~\citep{lambert2020objective,bhamidipaty2026imperfect}, proposal
coverage~\citep{imwm2026}, action grounding~\citep{acid2026,chen2026atm}, and
cost geometry~\citep{trm2026,straightening2026} each claim to be the binding
constraint, on different systems and under different conditions. Success rate
cannot distinguish a bad encoder from a bad predictor, proposal distribution or
cost, and prediction error is measured on a distribution the planner never
visits. This proposal argues the disagreement is largely an artifact of
protocol, and supplies the instrument and experiments that would settle it.

\section{Failure surfaces and the methodological gap}
% ================================================================

We organise the problem around four \emph{failure surfaces}
(Table~\ref{tab:surfaces}). The distinguishing feature is the third column: for
each surface, the observation that would \emph{refute} attributing a failure to
it. A taxonomy without refutation conditions organises vocabulary; one with them
organises experiments. One asymmetry is load-bearing: S1 is the only surface
that can be closed \emph{by construction}, by supplying exact dynamics to the
candidates, which makes it the natural control condition for studying the other
three.

\begin{table}[!ht]
\centering\small
\caption{Four failure surfaces, each with the condition that refutes attributing
a failure to it.}\label{tab:surfaces}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{2.35cm} L{4.6cm} Y@{}}
\toprule
Surface & Observable signature & What refutes an attribution to it \\
\midrule
S1. Prediction error & A physically good action sequence is rolled out
incorrectly & Failure persists when candidate dynamics are made exact \\
S2. Proposal coverage & No physically good candidate ever enters the population
& Good candidates are demonstrably present and are not selected \\
S3. Transition realizability & Predicted transitions are inconsistent with the
actions conditioning them & Failure persists when transitions are exact by
construction \\
S4. Cost geometry / selection & Low-proxy-cost candidates have poor physical
outcome; the gap widens with search & Proxy and physical rankings agree on the
optimiser's own realised populations \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

Reading the literature through this taxonomy exposes the gap that is the
analytical core of the proposal: \emph{nearly every published repair for S2, S3
or S4 is evaluated under learned dynamics}, where S1 is open. A gain measured
there cannot be attributed to the surface it claims to fix, because improving
any component correlated with prediction quality helps whenever prediction is
the binding constraint. The discriminating experiment --- rerunning the repair
with candidate dynamics held exact --- has, to our knowledge, not been reported
for any of them.

\section{Preliminary results: a controlled pilot}
% ================================================================

A completed pilot shows the gap is consequential rather than pedantic. In
tabletop Franka manipulation, candidate dynamics are made exact by snapshotting
simulator state at each replanning step, executing every candidate and encoding
the true rendered outcome, with encoder, optimiser, budget and horizon held
fixed so that only the terminal cost varies. A privileged state-based cost then
solves \emph{push} in $64/64$ episodes while the latent $L_2$ cost used by
released systems solves $0/64$, and a supervised state readout with
$1.6$--$1.7$\,cm held-out decoding error --- inside the $5$\,cm success radius
--- reaches at most $5/64$ (Table~\ref{tab:pilot}).

\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule

\midrule
Simulator-state reference (control) & 64/64 & 49/64 & 64/64 & 49/64 \\
Latent $L_2$ (as deployed) & 0/64 & 0/64 & 0/64 & 0/64 \\

Conditioning on the optimiser explains why an apparently accurate readout fails.
Readout and physical rankings agree moderately on initial candidate populations
(Spearman $0.43$--$0.55$) and collapse on the populations the optimiser itself
produces after refitting ($0.11$--$0.16$), and the excess selection regret over
a residual-matched permutation null exceeds the optimizer's-curse
baseline~\citep{smith2006optimizer} with all confidence intervals above zero.
Eight interventions run in the same harness --- off-policy-robust readouts,
relearned adapters, LoRA fine-tuning to $94$--$100\%$ grounding accuracy,
ensemble-disagreement penalties, a grounded dynamics channel, amortised
search-free control --- each improved the fidelity quantity it targeted, and
none moved contact planning outside the baseline band.

\section{Research questions}
% ================================================================

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{RQ\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.6em]
\item \textbf{Which failure surface binds where?} The diagnostic literature's
apparent disagreement may be an artifact of protocol --- different systems,
tasks and dynamics conditions --- rather than a real disagreement about
mechanism. Running the field's existing S3 and S4 repairs under exact candidate
dynamics would settle, for each, whether its reported gain survives when
prediction error is removed.
\item \textbf{Can a cost or representation be trained on the distribution the
planner itself induces?} Every objective in the representation literature is fit
on a distribution chosen by the experimenter; what a planner consumes is an
\emph{ordering} on a distribution chosen by the \emph{optimiser}, after its
search distribution has refitted. Whether an objective can be defined directly
on that induced distribution is open, and the pilot's recurring pattern ---
fidelity improves, planning does not --- suggests it is the consequential
question.
\item \textbf{Does the mechanism generalise beyond one optimiser and one
representation family?} The pilot evidence comes from CEM and two frozen-encoder
checkpoints. Because the mechanism depends on distribution refitting, it should
transfer to MPPI and iCEM and behave differently under gradient-based and
amortised control --- a prediction, not yet a finding. The strongest competing
explanation, that frozen features lack the spatial precision contact
requires~\citep{murlabadia2026vjepa21}, must be tested in the same harness,
since the two imply opposite research programmes.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Significance, impact, and originality}
% ================================================================

\paragraph{Scientific significance} The proposal supplies what the field lacks:
a way to say \emph{why} a visual planner failed. Refutation conditions turn an
informal vocabulary of failure into experiments with defined outcomes, making
the literature's competing diagnoses commensurable for the first time.

\paragraph{Corrective impact on an active literature} If the pilot's finding
generalises, a body of reported gains is misattributed: repairs credited to
coverage, grounding or cost geometry may be repairing prediction error. Either
outcome is informative --- a repair that loses its benefit under exact dynamics
was repairing S1; one that survives is a counterexample --- and both redirect
effort now spent on the wrong surface.

\paragraph{Practical impact on robot learning} The evidence points at a widely
shared design assumption --- that a representation faithful on a fixed dataset
yields a cost usable by search --- and shows it failing where manipulation is
hardest: contact-rich tasks with centimetre-scale success criteria. Objectives
defined on rankings within the populations a planner realises are the natural
response and are untested at scale; and because the diagnosis targets the cost
rather than the backbone, any resulting method composes with the frozen-encoder
checkpoints the field already trains at great expense.

\paragraph{Methodological impact} Three quantities are cheap to compute and
essentially never reported: candidate availability, selection regret against the
best available candidate, and proxy--physical rank correlation at the first and
last search iteration. Standardising them makes negative results interpretable,
and is a contribution independent of any new method.

\paragraph{Originality} Three elements are, to our knowledge, new: refutation
conditions attached to a failure taxonomy; the exact-dynamics control used as a
general instrument for attribution rather than a one-off ablation; and measuring
proxy--physical agreement \emph{on the optimiser's own realised populations}
rather than on a fixed evaluation set --- where the pilot's agreement collapses
and the mechanism becomes visible.

\section{Methodology and work plan}
% ================================================================

The programme is a sequence of controlled comparisons in which one component
varies and every other is pinned. Three commitments recur: (i)~\emph{exact
candidate dynamics} as a control, closing S1 by construction; (ii)~\emph{matched
harnesses}, holding encoder, optimiser, budget, horizon and success criterion
identical across arms; and (iii)~\emph{reporting conditioned on the optimiser},
measuring agreement and regret on realised populations at both the first and the
final search iteration. Contact-rich and free-space cells are reported
separately, because pooled suite-level rates conceal complete failure on the
contact subset.

\vspace{2pt}\noindent\textbf{WP1 --- Discriminating reruns of existing repairs (RQ1).}~ Reimplement representative S3 and S4 repairs (inverse-consistency and
action-grounding costs, reachability heads, learned terminal metrics) in the
shared harness. For each: reproduce its reported gain under \emph{learned}
dynamics as a positive control, then rerun it unchanged under \emph{exact}
candidate dynamics. Outcome: a per-repair statement of how much of the gain
survives removal of prediction error.

\vspace{2pt}\noindent\textbf{WP2 --- Selection-aware objectives (RQ2).}~ Develop costs and representations trained on the distribution the planner
induces. Two families are compared against scalar regression onto true cost:
group-relative \emph{ranking} objectives over realised candidate populations,
and a \emph{decision-equivalence} criterion asking the latent to preserve the
$\arg\min$ over a family of goals --- far weaker than reconstructing state, far
stronger than predicting the next latent, and the reward-free analogue of
TD-MPC2's value supervision~\citep{hansen2024tdmpc2}. Training uses populations
harvested from planner rollouts, refreshed as the induced distribution shifts.

\vspace{2pt}\noindent\textbf{WP3 --- Generality and the competing explanation (RQ3).}~ Two sweeps in the same harness: (i)~representation families, including a
dense-feature checkpoint~\citep{murlabadia2026vjepa21} that no published study
has run through a cost-attribution protocol, adjudicating between the
cost-geometry and representation-precision accounts; and (ii)~optimisers --- CEM,
MPPI, iCEM and an amortised controller --- testing whether the failure tracks
distribution refitting. Hierarchical decomposition~\citep{zhang2026hwm} is a
stretch arm.

\vspace{2pt}\noindent\textbf{WP4 --- Evaluation protocol and release.}~ Formalise the three reporting quantities and ship the harness,
the exact-dynamics control and the diagnostic suite as an open benchmark with
per-regime reporting; developed continuously alongside WP1--WP3.

\centering\small
\caption{Indicative schedule for a two-year Master's programme; WP4 runs

\toprule

\paragraph{Scope} WP1 and WP2 are the core and are sized to complete in full:
the pilot harness, checkpoints and simulator infrastructure already exist,
removing the setup cost that would otherwise dominate year one. WP3's hierarchy
arm can be dropped without loss to the thesis argument. Claims are stated at the
scope the evidence supports: two checkpoint lineages and two task families, not
latent planning in general.

\paragraph{Threats to validity} A null result under exact dynamics is
uninterpretable without a reproduced positive control, so every WP1 arm carries
one. Seed variance is real --- one pilot arm produced $5/16$ against a
$0$--$2/16$ band --- so claims are made over paired environment seeds with
clustered bootstrap intervals, and over checkpoint training seeds where
feasible. Where an intervention is null, the targeted quantity is reported
alongside the planning outcome, so that ``the repair did not work'' stays
distinguishable from ``the repair did not engage''.

\section{Resources and facilities required}
% ================================================================

The programme is computationally moderate: it trains no large backbone, and
operates on released frozen checkpoints, small probes and terminal-cost heads.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Compute.} A GPU cluster with 40--80\,GB-class accelerators (H100
or equivalent). The exact-dynamics condition dominates, since every candidate is
executed and rendered --- roughly $10^{5}$ simulator rollouts per experimental
cell. Indicatively 8--12 GPUs in bursts with SLURM-style batch scheduling; the
pilot ran at this scale.
\item \textbf{Simulation and storage.} MuJoCo-based manipulation suites
(MetaWorld~\citep{yu2020metaworld} and a second contact-rich suite) with
privileged state access, required for the positive control and for regime
stratification; 5--10\,TB for cached latents, candidate populations and logs.
\item \textbf{Datasets and checkpoints.} Publicly released world-model
checkpoints across scales and lineages~\citep{zhou2024dinowm,jepawms2025,
assran2025vjepa2,murlabadia2026vjepa21}, plus an offline real-robot dataset for
the transfer check. No new data collection is required.
\item \textbf{Robot hardware (optional).} A Franka-class arm with wrist and
third-person cameras would allow closed-loop confirmation, but is desirable
rather than necessary: the exact-dynamics control is unavailable on real
hardware, so the core experiments are inherently simulator-based and the arm
serves only the final transfer check, for which an offline dataset substitutes.
\item \textbf{Supervision.} Supervision spanning self-supervised representation
learning and model-based control, since the central hypothesis lives at their
interface. No fieldwork, human subjects or restricted data are involved.
\end{itemize}
